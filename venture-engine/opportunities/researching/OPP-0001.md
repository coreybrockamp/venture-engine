# Opportunity - OPP-0001

## One-Sentence Thesis

A bounded human-guided care-arrangement workflow may help an adult child organize next steps after a parent’s care needs abruptly increase.

## Persona

PER-001 adult child managing an aging parent.

## Problem

PROB-0004 - care capacity and paid/unpaid trade-offs.

## User / Buyer / Beneficiary

User: adult child. Buyer: adult child, parent, or shared family payment (**UNKNOWN**). Beneficiary: parent and family caregiver.

## Trigger

Hospital discharge, sudden decline, or caregiver-capacity failure.

## Desired Outcome

A documented near-term arrangement with named owners and visible coverage gaps.

## Solution Hypothesis

**HYPOTHESIS:** a non-clinical productized human-plus-workflow service organizes the first care-arrangement decisions and routes specialist questions outward.

## Value Proposition

Reduce the time spent reconstructing responsibilities during a high-stakes transition.

## Current Alternative

Family calls, spreadsheets, local agencies, Wellthy, or doing it alone.

## Existing Market Evidence

OBS-000013, OBS-000015, OBS-000016, and OBS-000031 show transition and capacity burden. Wellthy and Visiting Angels establish paid alternatives.

## Competitors / Substitutes

COMP-0005 Wellthy; COMP-0006 Visiting Angels.

## Business Model

Productized service with optional recurring coordination support.

## Pricing Hypothesis

**FACT:** Wellthy lists US$400-475/month private access. **HYPOTHESIS:** a narrower workflow could be paid; price is UNKNOWN.

## Distribution Hypothesis

Google Search and caregiver communities match discharge and capacity triggers.

## Why Now

**FACT:** AARP/NAC reports 63 million U.S. family caregivers. **UNKNOWN:** whether that creates a new purchase window.

## Evidence For

Repeated capacity trade-offs and existing paid human services.

## Evidence Against

The need may be local or episodic; incumbents may already serve the highest-value cases.

## Key Assumptions

Adult children seek help immediately; a bounded workflow is distinct; families trust a non-clinical service; the buyer can pay.

## Biggest Risk

Existing agencies and human concierge services may be sufficient.

## What Must Be True

Families prefer a bounded transition service to free guidance or a broad concierge.

## Cheapest Question To Test Later

Would a recent care-transition caregiver pay for bounded non-clinical arrangement help?
