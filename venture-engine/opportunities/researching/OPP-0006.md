# Opportunity - OPP-0006

## One-Sentence Thesis

An FSM-connected dispatch-exception queue may help an owner or dispatcher decide what to change first when weather, job overruns, or missing field information disrupt an otherwise configured schedule.

## Persona

PER-003 — local service-business owner with roughly 5–25 employees.

## Problem

PROB-0005 — crew schedule and field/office coordination.

## Residual Pain Classification

**B. POORLY SOLVED (HYPOTHESIS).** FSM products cover scheduling and dispatch, yet a Jobber co-owner still manually orders routes during surprise storms and a Service Fusion owner reports a field-view gap. The evidence does not establish prevalence after competent implementation.

## Why Existing FSM Does Not Fully Solve It

**FACT:** Jobber, Housecall Pro, ServiceTitan, Workiz, and FieldPulse provide scheduling and dispatch. **HYPOTHESIS:** a disruption still leaves a human to prioritize conflicting jobs, crews, and incomplete information; this is a decision queue, not another schedule calendar.

## User / Buyer / Beneficiary

User: dispatcher or owner. Buyer: owner/operator (**HYPOTHESIS**). Beneficiary: owner, office, and field crew.

## Trigger

A weather event, late-running job, no-show, incomplete field information, or other same-day schedule exception.

## Economic Consequence

**HYPOTHESIS:** unresolved exceptions consume owner/office attention and can reduce reliable crew deployment. Lost utilization or revenue is **UNKNOWN**.

## Desired Outcome

A prioritized, explainable next action for the schedule exception without rebuilding the day manually.

## Solution Hypothesis

**HYPOTHESIS:** an integration that ingests existing FSM job, crew, and status data; flags exception conflicts; and presents a human-approved reallocation recommendation with the missing information that blocks action.

## Product Classification

ADD-ON / INTEGRATION. It does not replace the system of record.

## Value Proposition

Reduce the manual decision work of coordinating a disrupted schedule, while retaining the incumbent FSM for core dispatch.

## Current Alternative

Dispatcher or owner manually reorders routes, calls/texts crews, checks the FSM board, or absorbs the disruption.

## Competitors / Substitutes

Jobber (COMP-0007), Housecall Pro (COMP-0008), ServiceTitan (COMP-0009), Workiz (COMP-0010), FieldPulse (COMP-0011), plus dispatcher labor and existing FSM scheduling views.

## Existing Market Evidence

**FACT:** OBS-000063 describes manual route ordering during surprise storms despite Jobber use; OBS-000065 describes daily dispatch use with an incomplete installer phone view. The market analysis confirms all named incumbents cover core scheduling. No record proves a buyer would pay separately for a decision layer.

## Business Model

**HYPOTHESIS:** recurring add-on subscription per operating team, contingent on proven incremental value.

## Pricing Hypothesis

**FACT:** Jobber's annual-billed plans span US$29–399/month and Housecall Pro starts at US$59/month annual-billed; other incumbent pricing is per-seat or quote-based. **UNKNOWN:** willingness to add a separate subscription; no price is selected.

## Distribution Hypothesis

**HYPOTHESIS:** FSM integration marketplaces and trade/owner communities could reach buyers during a disruption or scheduling-fit complaint, but channel access and conversion are unvalidated.

## Evidence For

- **FACT:** a paid Jobber user manually orders routes during surprise storms (OBS-000063).
- **FACT:** a paid dispatch user reports an incomplete field mobile view (OBS-000065).
- **FACT:** dispatch is a recurring, commercially validated job (PROB-0005 market memo).

## Evidence Against

- **FACT:** every reviewed FSM incumbent markets scheduling, dispatch, and mobile job workflows.
- **FACT:** some users report paid systems improve operations; the evidence does not show a widespread post-implementation gap.
- **UNKNOWN:** separate willingness to pay, measurable utilization effect, and integration access.

## Key Assumptions

- Disruption decisions, not merely data entry, recur after competent FSM adoption.
- Existing FSM data is sufficient to make an actionable recommendation.
- Owner/dispatcher trusts a recommendation but retains approval.
- The avoided coordination work or capacity loss can be measured.
- An add-on is easier to adopt than a replacement.

## Biggest Commercial Risk

Incumbent feature expansion or existing dispatch views may be sufficient, leaving no reason to add another subscription.

## Differentiation Test

The owner would pay only if the product converts incumbent data into a prioritized exception decision that the schedule board, spreadsheets, and dispatcher judgment do not already provide adequately.

## What Must Be True

Post-adoption scheduling exceptions are frequent enough and costly enough that an owner values a separate, integration-based decision layer.

## Cheapest Question To Test Later

When a same-day disruption occurs in a configured FSM, what exact decision requires manual reconstruction and what outcome changes if it is delayed?

## Sources

OBS-000063, OBS-000065; COMP-0007 through COMP-0011; PROB-0005 market-analysis memo.

## Status

Researching; unscored; no strategic decision; no experiment designed.
