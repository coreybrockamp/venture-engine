# Opportunity - OPP-0008

## One-Sentence Thesis

A fixed-scope FSM implementation-readiness and configuration service may help a growing local service-business owner choose, clean up, and operationalize an existing system without a disruptive, open-ended migration.

## Persona

PER-003 — local service-business owner with roughly 5–25 employees.

## Problem

PROB-0007 — field-service systems-fit gap.

## Residual Pain Classification

**C. IMPLEMENTATION-DEPENDENT.** The evidence supports software evaluation, onboarding, cost, and fit friction, but does not establish a missing FSM tier. This hypothesis is a service around adoption, not a new platform.

## Why Existing FSM Does Not Fully Solve It

**FACT:** vendors cover the functional workflow and offer tiers; ServiceTitan documents staged onboarding, while FieldPulse documents paid migration and customer preparation obligations. **HYPOTHESIS:** a 5–25-person owner can still lack a neutral, bounded way to choose the right workflow, prepare data, and configure team adoption.

## User / Buyer / Beneficiary

User: owner/operator and designated office lead. Buyer: owner/operator (**HYPOTHESIS**). Beneficiary: owner, office, and field team.

## Trigger

Headcount growth, a renewal/user-cost increase, impending software switch, or a stalled implementation.

## Economic Consequence

**HYPOTHESIS:** poor selection or implementation can prolong manual work, add migration/rework cost, and disrupt operations. Typical migration cost, ROI, and completion rates are **UNKNOWN**.

## Desired Outcome

A configured incumbent FSM with a documented workflow, clean migration inputs, clear role adoption, and a defined handoff to the owner.

## Solution Hypothesis

**HYPOTHESIS:** a fixed-scope, vendor-neutral readiness and configuration service: workflow mapping, data-cleanup checklist, platform-selection constraints, configuration plan, and adoption handoff. It would not resell or replace an FSM.

## Product Classification

SERVICE. Any software support is secondary to a bounded implementation engagement.

## Value Proposition

Reduce avoidable selection and implementation overhead while preserving the chosen incumbent system.

## Current Alternative

Vendor onboarding, paid migration, office/owner self-implementation, consultants, employee-admin labor, or postponing the switch.

## Competitors / Substitutes

Vendor onboarding and professional services from Jobber (COMP-0007), Housecall Pro (COMP-0008), ServiceTitan (COMP-0009), Workiz (COMP-0010), and FieldPulse (COMP-0011), plus independent consultants and internal staff.

## Existing Market Evidence

**FACT:** OBS-000058 reports complex ServiceTitan setup; OBS-000062 captures user-cost pressure prompting alternative consideration; COMP-0009 documents formal onboarding and COMP-0011 documents paid migration/configuration obligations. **FACT:** current tiers and onboarding may already adequately solve most implementations.

## Business Model

**HYPOTHESIS:** fixed-fee, narrowly scoped service engagement with explicit deliverables; price is unknown.

## Pricing Hypothesis

**FACT:** ServiceTitan separately invoices professional services when ordered; FieldPulse documents paid data migration. **UNKNOWN:** what an independent readiness/configuration service can charge or whether a 5–25-person owner prefers it to vendor services.

## Distribution Hypothesis

**HYPOTHESIS:** software-comparison search, trade communities, bookkeeping/QuickBooks advisors, and local FSM consultants may reach owners at a switch trigger; channel economics are unvalidated.

## Evidence For

- **FACT:** software comparison and growth-triggered selection behavior are observed (OBS-000044, OBS-000046, OBS-000047, OBS-000051).
- **FACT:** a small-business reviewer describes complex setup (OBS-000058).
- **FACT:** vendors document onboarding, migration, and paid professional-service work (COMP-0009, COMP-0011).

## Evidence Against

- **FACT:** established vendors already provide plan ladders, trials, onboarding, and migration support.
- **FACT:** the systems-fit evidence may be ordinary segmentation rather than a service gap.
- **UNKNOWN:** repeated independent willingness to pay for a neutral service and a repeatable scope.

## Key Assumptions

- Owners perceive implementation risk before purchase or renewal.
- A vendor-neutral scope has value beyond vendor onboarding.
- The engagement can be standardized across multiple trades.
- Clean data and workflow definition materially improve adoption.
- Owners will pay a fixed fee rather than self-implement or use vendor services.

## Biggest Commercial Risk

Vendor onboarding or internal implementation may be adequate, leaving insufficient willingness to pay for an independent service.

## Differentiation Test

The owner would use the service only if it reduces a selection/configuration decision and migration risk that vendor onboarding, internal staff, and a generic consultant do not resolve with equal confidence.

## What Must Be True

There is a repeatable, costly pre-implementation decision and cleanup burden that a bounded neutral service can solve better than existing vendor services.

## Cheapest Question To Test Later

Before adopting or switching an FSM, which preparation or configuration task do owners pay someone to handle, and why is vendor onboarding insufficient?
