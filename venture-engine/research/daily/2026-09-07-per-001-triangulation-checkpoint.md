## C. Strongest real market pains

1. **Care capacity and paid/unpaid trade-offs:** repeated full-time work, payment-structure questions, a current family-funded arrangement, and work constraints; evidence includes Tier 1 and Tier 2 signals.
2. **Fragmented parent administration:** late bills, insurance/coverage failures, medication follow-up, and financial consequences recur across independent sources.
3. **Healthcare information and task coordination:** independent reports describe multi-provider tasks, remote/local handoffs, and a direct request for shared information tooling.
4. **Uneven family workload:** recurring solo task ownership and conflict appear beyond Reddit, but evidence has not shown that software or a shared process would repair the underlying relationship problem.

## D. Weak or overstated pains

- Reddit likely overstated the frequency and universality of sibling conflict; cross-source support exists, but is still limited and condition-specific in the forum evidence.
- Coordination pain does not prove a new tool is needed: a shared Google spreadsheet and password manager were reported as helpful, and portals/autopay may solve narrower cases.
- Search-result patterns show discoverability, not query volume, customer identity, purchase intent, or willingness to pay.

## F. Commercial-signal review

- **Money already spent / proposed:** one family-funded paid caregiver arrangement; a $1,000/month requested in-home-care contribution; several inquiries about caregiver compensation; an out-of-pocket prescription consequence.
- **Expensive manual alternatives:** adult children and siblings manually handle bills, medication lists, insurance, appointments, grocery coordination, and daily check-ins. One family used a spreadsheet plus a password manager.
- **Active help-seeking:** payment-structure questions, an elder-law consultation, a request for a simple information-sharing app, and a home-adaptation provider search.
- **Poor current solutions:** pill reminders, manual lists, and task splitting did not prevent every failure; however, the record does not establish that existing tools are broadly inadequate.

## G. Remaining evidence gaps

- No representative prevalence, frequency measurement, or validated willingness-to-pay estimate.
- No proof of paid adoption of a coordination tool, nor evidence of which specific workflow families would replace.
- Alzheimer’s/memory-care cases are overrepresented in the new forum data; broader aging-parent contexts need deliberate sampling.
- Public Q&A was accessible only through its listing page; detailed linked pages were not reliably retrievable.
- Search-intent access provided result patterns but not query volume or autocomplete data.
