## Commercial Signals

### Existing Spend

**FACT:** Owners report a US$60 consultation (OBS-000089), a US$1,600 seven-hour quote (OBS-000090), US$150 weekly behaviorist work (OBS-000091), more than US$900 for a failed course (OBS-000093), US$1,100–2,400 program evaluation (OBS-000096), a US$1,200 lesson package (OBS-000097), paid apps/courses (OBS-000108, OBS-000110, OBS-000111), and premium reactive-dog boarding (OBS-000098). Prices are individual, jurisdiction-specific reports—not market averages.

### Active Solution Seeking and Professional-Service Use

**FACT:** Owners actively compare self-training and trainers, seek specialized classes or behavior support, and search for trusted boarding/care arrangements (OBS-000090, OBS-000096, OBS-000100, OBS-000103, OBS-000105, OBS-000107).

### Failed Prior Solutions

**FACT:** The evidence includes a low-value paid consultation, a US$900+ course described as counterproductive, a long plateau despite training/trainer spend, and a course reviewer who had tried multiple trainers/methods (OBS-000089, OBS-000093–000094, OBS-000112). Treatment quality and clinical cause are **UNKNOWN**.

### Recurring Management Burden

**FACT:** Owners describe logging incidents, triggers, distance, recovery, and training history; difficulty measuring progress; hard daily management; and use of family/trainer routines around a dog’s needs (OBS-000088, OBS-000091–000092, OBS-000095, OBS-000102, OBS-000109).

### Lifestyle and Economic Consequences

**FACT:** Travel may require specialized boarding, trusted known caregivers, driving with the dog, trial stays, or foregoing travel; one owner described limiting visits, restaurants, and ordinary activities to manage reactions (OBS-000095, OBS-000098–000101). These are individual cases and likely skew severe.

## Contradictory Evidence

- **FACT:** a trainer-supplied spreadsheet reportedly helped an owner see progress (OBS-000092).
- **FACT:** one owner reported substantial benefit from a US$1,200 private-lesson package (OBS-000097).
- **FACT:** low-cost self-guided material plus consistent practice was reported as helpful by one forum participant (OBS-000104).
- **FACT:** owners and reviewers also describe improvement through existing private lessons, group classes, apps, and courses (OBS-000108, OBS-000110–000112).

These records mean a future problem hypothesis must distinguish access, continuity, measurement, and fit gaps from cases that current training already solves.

## Source Quality Limitations and Bias Risks

Public communities and reviews are self-selected, may overrepresent severe reactivity, frustrated owners, treatment failures, and highly engaged researchers, and do not establish prevalence. Review pages may be incentivized or promotional. Income, geography, clinical history, trainer credentials, behavior severity, outcome durability, and spending recurrence are often unknown. No medical interpretation is made from medication mentions.

## Evidence Gaps

- Typical-case frequency and outcomes outside support-seeking communities.
- Recurring spend, retention, and total cost across training, veterinary behavior, boarding, and apps.
- Comparable evidence from owners whose dogs improve without intensive help.
- Clear owner willingness to pay beyond professional training, courses, or existing apps.
- Whether shared tracking or care coordination is a repeated owner problem rather than an enthusiast preference.
