## Commercial Signals

### Existing Software Spend

**FACT:** Households report paying for account aggregation and budgeting software, including a reported US$8/month premium app (OBS-000114), annual Monarch use (OBS-000127), paid Simplifi use after app research (OBS-000135), and a reported US$15/month YNAB subscription (OBS-000136). These are individual reports, not category conversion or retention data.

### Human-Service Spend

**FACT:** Two HENRY-context households reported paying a CPA after assembling records: one reported about US$3,500 after compiling business/rental P&Ls (OBS-000121); another reported US$1,500 while using a spreadsheet to prepare an overview (OBS-000122). The records show document-preparation work coexisting with paid professional service; they do not establish payment for a standalone organization service.

### Recurring Admin Burden

**FACT:** The corpus includes repeated monthly spreadsheet updates, annual reviews, separate account/card sheets, tax-document checklists, account inventories, and recurring statement review (OBS-000116, OBS-000123, OBS-000131–000134). Account aggregation failures can reintroduce manual entry, login, and reconciliation work (OBS-000127, OBS-000129–000130, OBS-000136).

### Financial Consequence

**FACT:** One forum participant described discovering a previously received form was omitted before creating a tax-document inventory (OBS-000132); another described an omitted number costing several hundred dollars after a software handoff (OBS-000134). These are individual, workflow-related consequences, not estimates of typical financial loss.

### Couple / Household Coordination

**FACT:** Households describe mixed joint/separate accounts, one partner operating finances, partial account access, annual or semiannual review meetings, and a spouse unable to locate accounts or passwords without a run book (OBS-000115–000118, OBS-000124–000126, OBS-000128). Some households report shared tools successfully provide full visibility (OBS-000128).

### Advisor Handoffs

**FACT:** Households prepare spreadsheets, expense categorization, P&Ls, prior returns, and shared tax folders for CPA or possible-preparer handoffs (OBS-000121–000123, OBS-000134). The data does not assess advisor quality or tax decisions.

### Active Solution Seeking

**FACT:** Participants actively requested tools for a 15-account household, an overall monthly financial-life view, and a shared-but-separated account configuration (OBS-000113, OBS-000119, OBS-000126).

### Privacy / Trust Barriers

**FACT:** Participants cited concern about connecting accounts to aggregators, login/password management, and recurring MFA or sync failures (OBS-000113, OBS-000115, OBS-000127, OBS-000136). **UNKNOWN:** how often privacy concern prevents adoption in the target persona.

## Strongest Commercial Pain Signals

1. **Account/data reliability after paid adoption:** paid users report manual reauthentication, duplicate transactions, manual support follow-up, and manual entry when aggregation fails (OBS-000127, OBS-000129–000130, OBS-000136).
2. **Recurring high-complexity record assembly:** HENRY-context households maintain multiple sheets, account inventories, and recurring reviews across employment, property, retirement, insurance, and household accounts (OBS-000116, OBS-000119, OBS-000123).
3. **Paid professional handoff still requires administration:** households report spending time compiling categorized records and overviews before paying a CPA (OBS-000121–000122).
4. **Household continuity risk:** one spouse can own account, credential, and document knowledge, creating a concrete handoff task (OBS-000115, OBS-000117, OBS-000131, OBS-000134).
5. **Mixed ownership is not automatically solved:** participants seek a system that preserves personal-account boundaries while creating household visibility (OBS-000118, OBS-000124–000126).

## Contradictory Evidence

- **FACT:** A paid app user reported that two household members could keep account ownership while viewing a complete shared picture (OBS-000128).
- **FACT:** Several households report that Monarch, Simplifi, a shared app, or a well-maintained spreadsheet produces sufficient visibility (OBS-000114, OBS-000116, OBS-000125, OBS-000135, OBS-000137).
- **FACT:** A forum participant described consolidating to one bank, autopay, paperless records, and a small retained document set, eliminating the need for a spreadsheet (OBS-000133).
- **FACT:** Some aggregation failure reflects institution connection behavior rather than an unmet household workflow (OBS-000127, OBS-000136–000137).

## Source Quality Limitations and Bias Risks

HENRY/community membership is not verified income, and some high-income claims are self-reported or second-hand. Financially sophisticated communities can overrepresent complex portfolios, optimization behavior, and users willing to manage spreadsheets. App-store and review-site evidence can overrepresent both enthusiasts and dissatisfied users; platform/institution connections vary by country and account type. The batch establishes concrete workflows, not prevalence, typical savings, market size, or willingness to pay for a new organization product.

## Evidence Gaps

- Verified high-income applicability beyond community context and self-report.
- Recurring spend or willingness to pay specifically for organization rather than advice, tax preparation, or a general finance app.
- Frequency and measurable time/financial savings of the residual work after a reliable existing setup.
- Whether couples will share financial data and what privacy boundaries they require.
- Whether advisor/CPA document handoff is a repeatable, separately purchasable workflow or ordinary client preparation.
- Whether tool failures are sufficiently common and cross-product to constitute a durable market gap.
