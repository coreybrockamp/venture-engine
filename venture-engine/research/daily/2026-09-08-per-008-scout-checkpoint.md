## Observed themes (not formal Problem clusters)

- **Commitment, 1:1, and follow-through work (7 observations):** A manager with four reports described losing track of a development commitment for about a month ([OBS-000138](../../data/observations.jsonl)); managers also described recurring 1:1 preparation, notes, priorities, and follow-up. Lightweight shared documents, templates, cadence, and a spreadsheet are repeatedly cited as workable alternatives ([OBS-000141](../../data/observations.jsonl), [OBS-000143](../../data/observations.jsonl), [OBS-000159](../../data/observations.jsonl)).
- **Delegation and accountability (3):** One manager reported delegation rework and feedback loops consuming roughly twice the time in the described case ([OBS-000139](../../data/observations.jsonl)). Counterevidence frames gradual assignment, check-ins, review, and feedback as a workable management practice rather than a missing tool ([OBS-000140](../../data/observations.jsonl)).
- **Performance feedback and follow-up (5):** First-time managers sought help for difficult feedback, missed deadlines, appraisal responsibility, and their first performance conversation ([OBS-000144](../../data/observations.jsonl), [OBS-000145](../../data/observations.jsonl), [OBS-000148](../../data/observations.jsonl)). The evidence does not yet distinguish a recurring, separate workflow from context-specific judgment, HR process, and senior-manager support.
- **Training-to-practice transition (6):** New managers reported uneven access to formal mentorship, coaching, or training ([OBS-000146](../../data/observations.jsonl), [OBS-000152](../../data/observations.jsonl)); book, course, and accelerator use show existing category behavior ([OBS-000151](../../data/observations.jsonl), [OBS-000157](../../data/observations.jsonl), [OBS-000158](../../data/observations.jsonl)). These are not evidence of willingness to pay for a new product.
- **Team visibility (1):** A manager reported using short weekly activity logs to learn roles and reduce knowledge concentration ([OBS-000142](../../data/observations.jsonl)); this is a simple-process counterexample.

## Paid behavior and buyer/payer evidence

**FACT:** Employer-provided mentoring, training, coaching, and HR programs appear in manager accounts ([OBS-000146](../../data/observations.jsonl), [OBS-000152](../../data/observations.jsonl)). A named management tool (Taito AI), 15Five, management books, and paid courses/programs were reported in use ([OBS-000147](../../data/observations.jsonl), [OBS-000151](../../data/observations.jsonl), [OBS-000155](../../data/observations.jsonl), [OBS-000157](../../data/observations.jsonl), [OBS-000158](../../data/observations.jsonl)).

**UNKNOWN:** The records do not establish who selected or paid for Taito AI, 15Five, courses, or coaching in a first-time-manager context. Employer category spend and employee use must not be treated as willingness to pay for a separate offering.

## Incumbent, substitute, and counterevidence

**Adequacy evidence:** shared documents and templates ([OBS-000141](../../data/observations.jsonl)); normal 1:1 cadence ([OBS-000143](../../data/observations.jsonl)); weekly logs ([OBS-000142](../../data/observations.jsonl)); a spreadsheet for performance clarity ([OBS-000159](../../data/observations.jsonl)); 15Five for structured practice and productive 1:1s ([OBS-000155](../../data/observations.jsonl)); and course/coaching accounts describing useful practical tools ([OBS-000157](../../data/observations.jsonl), [OBS-000158](../../data/observations.jsonl)).

**Failure/friction evidence:** forgotten commitments ([OBS-000138](../../data/observations.jsonl)); delegation rework ([OBS-000139](../../data/observations.jsonl)); difficult feedback and performance-conversation preparation ([OBS-000144](../../data/observations.jsonl), [OBS-000145](../../data/observations.jsonl)); uneven onboarding ([OBS-000146](../../data/observations.jsonl)); and manual priority retyping in 15Five ([OBS-000156](../../data/observations.jsonl)). The last item is one product-specific review and is not evidence of a broad residual job.

## Negative evidence and limitations

- Several sources present ordinary management practice, mentoring, HR involvement, templates, and existing tools as adequate; this weakens any assumption that pain alone indicates unmet software demand.
- Difficult feedback and underperformance management may be judgment-, policy-, and organization-specific rather than a standardized paid job.
- Reddit and Hacker News overrepresent online, often technology-adjacent management communities. App and course reviews are self-selected and the reviewer’s first-time-manager status is not always known.
- Source dates are unavailable on some community posts; all access dates and stable URLs are preserved in the structured records.

## Evidence gaps for a later, separately authorized review

- Independent confirmation that a recurring manager workflow remains unsolved after competent use of existing process, HR systems, management software, training, and coaching.
- Clear distinction among end user, solution selector, and economic buyer; especially direct evidence of first-time-manager-specific purchasing.
- Frequency, organizational context, and measurable impact beyond individual stressful events.
