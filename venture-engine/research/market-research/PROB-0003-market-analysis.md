# PROB-0003 Market Analysis - Uneven Family-Care Workload and Communication

## Executive Summary

**ASSESSMENT:** The burden is real and repeated, but a generic coordination product faces strong free substitutes and may not address the underlying family conflict.

## Problem Definition

Families struggle to make task ownership and updates visible when one person carries most parent-care work.

## Existing Customer Evidence

**FACT:** Five linked observations across four URLs describe a local caregiver absorbing recurring tasks, sibling nonparticipation, criticism, and burnout.

## Existing Solution Categories

Shared calendars, group texts, email, Google Sheets, CaringBridge, Lotsa Helping Hands, CareBridger, and sibling rotation.

## Competitor Landscape

CaringBridge provides private updates and requests for support; Lotsa Helping Hands is a free care calendar for meals, rides, tasks, and updates; CareBridger provides a shared-care platform.

## Current Alternatives

**FACT:** A Lotsa testimonial specifically reports six siblings using it to coordinate and communicate care for an elderly mother. These tools work for scheduling and visibility when participants agree to use them.

## Existing Spend

**WEAK:** Relevant direct substitutes are free. CareBridger publishes paid plans, but its early-access status means payment is not yet collected.

## Pricing Evidence

[Lotsa Helping Hands](https://lotsahelpinghands.com/) calls its service free; [CaringBridge](https://support.caringbridge.org/) describes a no-cost private page; [CareBridger](https://carebridger.com/pricing) lists future CA$9.99 and CA$18.99 paid tiers.

## Customer Complaints

The linked customer evidence is chiefly conflict and uneven work, not demonstrated failure of a particular coordination product.

## Search / Demand Signals

**WEAK:** a shared-caregiving-calendar query/result pattern exists, but no volume, comparison, or paid-intent measurement is available.

## Market Structure

**ASSESSMENT: COMPETITIVE at the free-tool layer, HIGH fragmentation overall.** Numerous generic and caregiving-specific tools cover scheduling and updates, while the relational problem remains outside their scope.

## Market Size

**UNKNOWN:** no paid addressable-market estimate is defensible from available evidence.

## Why Current Solutions Work

They are free, familiar, and can centralize assignments and reduce repeated updates.

## Why Current Solutions Fail

**FACT:** observations show nonparticipation and conflict. **UNKNOWN:** whether this is a tooling failure; no evidence shows software changes willingness to help.

## Evidence For Commercial Importance

Recurring care work and visible task ownership matter; multiple existing tools validate the coordination use case.

## Evidence Against Commercial Importance

Free alternatives are strong, and family conflict may require human, legal, or relational intervention rather than software.

## Key Unknowns

Whether families adopt shared tools, who invites nonparticipating relatives, and whether payment is plausible.

## Market Analyst Assessment

Existing spend: WEAK. Active demand: WEAK. Current solution satisfaction: MEDIUM. Market fragmentation: HIGH. Reachability: MEDIUM. Recurring need: HIGH. Commercial importance: MEDIUM. Confidence: 62.
