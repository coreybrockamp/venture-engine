# PROB-0004 Market Analysis - Care Capacity and Paid/Unpaid Care Trade-Offs

## Executive Summary

**ASSESSMENT:** This has the clearest existing spend, active help-seeking, and recurring economic consequences of the four problems. The landscape is fragmented and service-heavy, so the unmet need and buyer require further validation before opportunity generation.

## Problem Definition

Adult children need to make and coordinate care arrangements when unpaid family capacity, money, or stamina is insufficient.

## Existing Customer Evidence

**FACT:** Twelve linked observations across ten URLs include full-time care, payment-structure questions, an existing paid family-care arrangement, home-care decisions, and work constraints.

## Existing Solution Categories

Local home-care agencies, paid family caregivers, respite, care-concierge services, care managers, free logistics tools, family task rotation, and facilities.

## Competitor Landscape

Visiting Angels is a local in-home care agency network; Wellthy is a human care concierge; Lotsa Helping Hands covers free volunteer logistics; CareBridger covers care-circle coordination.

## Current Alternatives

**FACT:** Families use direct task rotation, hired caregivers, paid family arrangements, and free coordination tools. Human services work for physical tasks and exception handling but require availability and affordability.

## Existing Spend

**FACT:** Step 6 contains one current paid family-care arrangement and multiple compensation inquiries. Visiting Angels local pages publish US$28-36/hour in Coral Springs and US$40-48/hour agency care in Chaska; Wellthy lists US$475/month private access or US$400/month for three months.

## Pricing Evidence

Local home-care figures vary by location, hours, care level, and minimums; they are not national prices. See [Visiting Angels Coral Springs](https://www.visitingangels.com/coralsprings/home-care-costs), [Visiting Angels Chaska](https://www.visitingangels.com/chaska/articles/navigating-senior-home-care-costs-in-minnesota-a-guide-for-families/25402), and [Wellthy plans](https://www.wellthy.com/plans-details/).

## Customer Complaints

**FACT:** Existing evidence includes affordability, capacity, and coverage problems. A systematic agency-review sample was not accessible in this run, so service-quality complaint prevalence is UNKNOWN.

## Search / Demand Signals

**MODERATE:** public requests concern caregiver pay and home/service help, plus an established provider/concierge category. Query volume is unavailable.

## Market Structure

**ASSESSMENT: HIGH fragmentation.** Local agencies, payer programs, family arrangements, free logistics tools, and premium concierge services coexist; no single national direct solution was established.

## Market Size

**FACT:** AARP/NAC reports 63 million family caregivers and 11.2 million paid family caregivers. **UNKNOWN:** the subset needing additional capacity and their payer mix.

## Why Current Solutions Work

Human care can deliver physical assistance, transportation, respite, and exception handling; families can buy only required hours.

## Why Current Solutions Fail

**FACT:** Local price varies and quotes are often needed; observations show exhaustion and financial trade-offs. **UNKNOWN:** whether a gap is primarily affordability, discovery, trust, coverage, or coordination.

## Evidence For Commercial Importance

Direct service spend, a published private concierge price, paid family care, and recurring workload consequences.

## Evidence Against Commercial Importance

Need may be crisis-driven, local, regulated, and service-first; a family with adequate long-term-care resources is a counterexample to universal unmet affordability.

## Key Unknowns

Economic buyer, payer coverage, local supply, trust/acquisition channels, and whether families prefer human services to software.

## Market Analyst Assessment

Existing spend: STRONG. Active demand: MODERATE. Current solution satisfaction: MEDIUM. Market fragmentation: HIGH. Reachability: MEDIUM. Recurring need: HIGH. Commercial importance: HIGH. Confidence: 80.
