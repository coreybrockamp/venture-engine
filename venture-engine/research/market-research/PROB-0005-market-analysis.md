# PROB-0005 Market Analysis - Crew Schedule and Field/Office Coordination

## Executive Summary

**ASSESSMENT:** This is a recurring, commercially validated workflow, but the solution market is competitive. The evidence supports continued research into fit, implementation, and residual coordination failures—not an assumption that a new dispatch product is needed.

## Existing Customer Evidence

**FACT:** A 15-technician shop outgrew a shared email calendar (OBS-000053); an eight-route firm sought an alternative to using QuickBooks across routes and tickets (OBS-000051); a co-owner still manually orders recurring routes (OBS-000063); and a master-plumber owner uses dispatch daily but reports an incomplete mobile field view (OBS-000065).

## Current Workflow

Teams use general calendars or accounting tools before moving to field-service-management (FSM) systems. Dispatchers and owners then assign, reorder, and communicate job changes; mobile adoption and field/office data quality determine whether work is actually reduced.

## Existing Solution Categories

FSM suites; trade-specific route systems; QuickBooks plus calendars/texting; and manual office coordination.

## Competitor Landscape

Jobber, Housecall Pro, ServiceTitan, Workiz, and FieldPulse all market scheduling, dispatch, job data, and field-mobile workflows. They represent a competitive, fragmented set of direct alternatives rather than proof of an empty market.

## Pricing

**FACT:** Jobber publishes annual-billed starting tiers from US$29/month for one user through US$399/month including 15 users. Housecall Pro displays Basic at US$59/month annual-billed for one user and an Essentials configuration at US$149/month. ServiceTitan is per-technician, quote-only; Workiz is request-pricing; FieldPulse is customized seat-based pricing. See [Jobber](https://www.getjobber.com/pricing/), [Housecall Pro](https://www.housecallpro.com/pricing/), [ServiceTitan](https://www.servicetitan.com/get-pricing), [Workiz](https://www.workiz.com/pricing-plans/), and [FieldPulse](https://www.fieldpulse.com/pricing).

## Existing Spend

**STRONG category evidence:** OBS-000045, OBS-000048, OBS-000051, and OBS-000065 document use or active evaluation of paid CRM/FSM systems. Exact typical spend for the persona is UNKNOWN.

## Current Alternatives

Google Calendar, QuickBooks, spreadsheets, phone/texting, shared email, and route/dispatch software remain common because they are familiar, cheap, or already embedded in operations.

## Switching Behavior

**MODERATE:** owners evaluate products when adding staff, outgrowing a calendar, needing route/field visibility, or anticipating a future switch. Migration/implementation burden remains a constraint.

## Customer Complaints

Public evidence includes schedule complexity, manual route ordering, field-information visibility gaps, and implementation friction. It does not establish that these failures are common after competent setup.

## Economic Impact

**MODERATE, unquantified:** dispatch is daily/recurring and can consume office time or affect capacity. No source provides a verified utilization, missed-job, or labor-cost amount.

## Market Structure

**COMPETITIVE / HIGHLY FRAGMENTED:** broad FSM suites, specialized tools, and manual stacks address overlapping segments.

## Why Current Solutions Work

Paid suites consolidate dispatch, job information, invoicing, and field communication. Some users report substantial operational improvement.

## Why Current Solutions Fail

Fit varies by route pattern, team size, configuration, data quality, mobile needs, and the effort required to standardize processes.

## Evidence For Commercial Importance

Recurring dispatch use, active system evaluations, and broad paid product coverage validate a commercial job.

## Evidence Against Commercial Importance

Strong incumbents already cover the workflow; observed gaps may reflect poor fit or implementation rather than a durable unmet need.

## Key Unknowns

Which coordination task persists after a 5-25-person firm has competently implemented an FSM suite, and what measurable capacity or revenue consequence remains?

## Market Analyst Assessment

Existing spend: **STRONG**. Active demand: **MODERATE**. Switching evidence: **MODERATE**. Revenue/margin impact: **MODERATE**. Current solution satisfaction: **MEDIUM**. Market fragmentation: **HIGH**. Recurring need: **HIGH**. Commercial importance: **HIGH**. Confidence: **76**.
