# PROB-0006 Market Analysis - Quote-to-Cash Handoff Friction

## Executive Summary

**ASSESSMENT:** Estimate, invoice, payment, and follow-up workflows have clear owner-level economic relevance and are built into every reviewed FSM suite. The problem merits continued research because public evidence shows execution failures, but measurable leakage and a separate willingness to pay remain unproven.

## Existing Customer Evidence

**FACT:** A contractor reported twelve unsent estimates in a busy period (OBS-000049); another sought bid-follow-up help as backlog declined (OBS-000050); a service operator used handwritten field information for office invoicing (OBS-000057); and a review described mobile invoices requiring desktop rework to sync (OBS-000064).

## Current Workflow

Owners or office staff prepare estimates, follow up by phone/email, convert completed work to invoices, collect payment on site or online, and chase overdue invoices. Manual field-to-office handoffs create delay risk.

## Existing Solution Categories

FSM quote/invoice/payment suites; accounting software; payment processors; pricebooks; and manual email, phone, paper, and office-admin workflows.

## Competitor Landscape

Jobber, Housecall Pro, ServiceTitan, Workiz, and FieldPulse all position quote, invoice, payment, reminder, or pricebook capabilities as core workflow coverage. The market is competitive, with no evidence yet of a discrete unserved product category.

## Pricing

**FACT:** Jobber's US$99/month Connect tier includes automated quote/invoice follow-up and QuickBooks Online sync; Grow starts at US$149/month and adds job costing. Housecall Pro's displayed Basic plan starts at US$59/month annual-billed and includes quotes, invoices, and payments. FieldPulse's public payment terms list 3.00% card volume, 1.00% ACH volume, US$0.25 per item, and US$9 monthly access; its core subscription is quote/custom-seat based. See [Jobber](https://www.getjobber.com/pricing/), [Housecall Pro](https://www.housecallpro.com/pricing/), and [FieldPulse payment terms](https://www.fieldpulse.com/terms-of-service-payments).

## Existing Spend

**MODERATE:** linked records show payment/invoicing software adoption. Pricing shows owners can buy these capabilities bundled inside FSM subscriptions; standalone spend for this workflow is UNKNOWN.

## Current Alternatives

Manual follow-up, paper invoices, office-created invoices, QuickBooks, card processing, and integrated FSM systems.

## Switching Behavior

**WEAK to MODERATE:** owners seek tools for follow-up and evaluate integrated systems. Review evidence also cites invoice and sync limitations. It does not prove that firms switch specifically for quote-to-cash functionality.

## Customer Complaints

Estimate backlogs, manual invoicing, growing-client invoicing limitations, and sync rework appear in the evidence. They span sources but remain a small, non-representative sample.

## Economic Impact

**MODERATE:** slow estimates may lose a customer; late invoicing can delay collection; manual rework consumes office labor. **UNKNOWN:** dollar loss, close-rate impact, and cash-collection days.

## Market Structure

**COMPETITIVE:** core FSM, accounting, and payments products overlap heavily.

## Why Current Solutions Work

Reviewed suites offer automated follow-up, estimate-to-invoice conversion, online/on-site payments, reminders, pricebooks, and reporting. One HVAC reviewer reported less invoice chasing after adopting Jobber (OBS-000060).

## Why Current Solutions Fail

Incomplete implementation, field/mobile sync defects, disconnected accounting, or owner follow-through can leave the workflow manual despite feature availability.

## Evidence For Commercial Importance

Revenue conversion and cash collection are owner-critical; paid systems directly target them; active follow-up concern is documented.

## Evidence Against Commercial Importance

This may be an execution/discipline and implementation problem within existing suites, not a separate purchase category. No quantified owner loss or direct demand for another product is established.

## Key Unknowns

How often do 5-25-person firms lose revenue or cash because existing quote-to-cash tooling is insufficient rather than unused or poorly configured?

## Market Analyst Assessment

Existing spend: **MODERATE**. Active demand: **MODERATE**. Switching evidence: **WEAK**. Revenue/margin impact: **MODERATE**. Current solution satisfaction: **MEDIUM**. Market fragmentation: **HIGH**. Recurring need: **HIGH**. Commercial importance: **HIGH**. Confidence: **74**.
