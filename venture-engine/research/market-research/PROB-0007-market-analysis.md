# PROB-0007 Market Analysis - Field-Service Systems-Fit Gap

## Executive Summary

**ASSESSMENT:** There is clear existing spend, active comparison behavior, and real implementation/switching work. However, the current evidence cannot distinguish a structural 5-25-person market gap from normal software segmentation and buyer-specific fit. Carry forward for research, not as a confirmed market opening.

## Existing Customer Evidence

**FACT:** Records show owners/operators comparing systems as teams grow (OBS-000044, OBS-000046, OBS-000047, OBS-000051), a seven-truck firm reporting rising renewal fees (OBS-000055), complex onboarding (OBS-000058), and user-cost pressure prompting alternative consideration (OBS-000062).

## Current Workflow

Firms begin with general tools or a lower-tier FSM package, evaluate systems when headcount/workflows grow, then configure data, pricebooks, accounting, permissions, and mobile workflows. Switching may require migration, training, and operational change.

## Existing Solution Categories

Low-cost calendar/accounting/manual stacks; team-oriented FSM subscriptions; quote-based per-technician suites; and migration/onboarding services.

## Competitor Landscape

Jobber, Housecall Pro, Workiz, FieldPulse, and ServiceTitan span the same functional space with differing pricing transparency, included users, add-ons, and onboarding depth. This is evidence of segmentation and competition—not proof of a missing tier.

## Pricing

**FACT:** Jobber publicly starts at US$29/month for Core, US$99 for Connect (five users), US$149 for Grow (ten users), and US$399 for Plus (15 users), annual-billed. Housecall Pro displays US$59/month Basic for one user and US$149/month Essentials in its annual-billed configuration. ServiceTitan is per-technician but quote-only. Workiz lists tier names but requests pricing; FieldPulse uses customized seats and its public tool displays US$99/user in a configuration. [Jobber](https://www.getjobber.com/pricing/), [Housecall Pro](https://www.housecallpro.com/pricing/), [ServiceTitan](https://www.servicetitan.com/get-pricing), [Workiz](https://www.workiz.com/pricing-plans/), [FieldPulse](https://www.fieldpulse.com/pricing).

## Existing Spend

**STRONG:** paid subscriptions, renewals, and per-user/per-technician structures are documented. **UNKNOWN:** typical all-in spend, onboarding fee, contract term, and migration cost for a representative 5-25-person firm.

## Current Alternatives

QuickBooks, Google Calendar, spreadsheets, paper, lower-tier FSM plans, team-focused plans, and larger quote-based suites.

## Switching Behavior

**MODERATE:** growth, feature gaps, cost, and onboarding/fit concerns drive evaluation. FieldPulse documents paid data migration and configuration obligations; ServiceTitan documents staged onboarding. But actual completed switching rates are UNKNOWN.

## Customer Complaints

Public reviews cite pricing, feature limits, onboarding, integration, and navigation issues. Review sites may overrepresent selection/implementation feedback and include incentives, so they cannot prove prevalence.

## Economic Impact

**MODERATE:** subscription expense, renewal/user costs, migration work, and operational disruption can affect owner overhead and capacity. No verified typical migration cost or ROI is available.

## Market Structure

**COMPETITIVE / SEGMENTED:** published low-entry tiers and customized higher-complexity offerings create a broad ladder, not a binary simple-versus-enterprise market.

## Why Current Solutions Work

Low-to-mid-market tools publish team tiers and cover scheduling, estimates, invoices, payments, and follow-up; higher-capability suites provide deeper reporting and implementation support.

## Why Current Solutions Fail

Pricing may be opaque, user additions/add-ons can change cost, and data migration, process configuration, training, and accounting integration impose meaningful adoption work.

## Evidence For Commercial Importance

Software is a recurring paid category; independent owners actively compare systems; formal onboarding/migration programs validate switching work.

## Evidence Against Commercial Importance

The observed trade-off may be normal segmentation. Incumbents already provide plans for teams, upgrade paths, trials, and professional implementation; switching friction may suppress demand for another tool.

## Key Unknowns

Is there a repeatable 5-25-person segment that cannot obtain acceptable workflow fit at a sustainable total cost from current products, and what task remains unresolved after implementation?

## Market Analyst Assessment

Existing spend: **STRONG**. Active demand: **MODERATE**. Switching evidence: **MODERATE**. Revenue/margin impact: **MODERATE**. Current solution satisfaction: **MEDIUM**. Market fragmentation: **HIGH**. Recurring need: **MEDIUM**. Commercial importance: **MEDIUM**. Confidence: **78**.
