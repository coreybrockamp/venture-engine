# PROB-0008 Market Analysis — Scope-Control and Effective-Rate Leakage

## Executive Summary

**ASSESSMENT:** Scope leakage has a direct, commercially meaningful example and a high-fit six-figure retainer context, but the market is primarily process/template-led and already software-enabled. The current evidence does not establish a paid residual tool gap after competent proposal, contract, and change-control practice.

## Persona Fit

**HIGH:** one direct six-figure solo-freelancer retainer (OBS-000070). **MEDIUM:** a US$100/hour project-loss account and a writer/editor with six steady clients (OBS-000067, OBS-000071). Other support is low-fit peer or industry guidance. Conclusions therefore remain medium confidence.

## Existing Customer Evidence

**FACT:** OBS-000067 reports 23 hours of claimed unpaid expansion on a US$2,000 project. OBS-000070 describes version/runtime creep and recurring renegotiation on a just-over-six-figure retainer. OBS-000071 shows pricing pressure in an established six-client practice. Neither record establishes prevalence.

## Current Workflow

Consultants define scope and price in a proposal or agreement, perform delivery, receive revisions or changed requests, then either revise the estimate/contract and obtain approval or absorb the work. Deposits, revision limits, change orders, and explicit written approval are the documented controls.

## Existing Solution Categories

Process discipline and client communication; proposal/contract templates; integrated client suites; document/approval tools; contract/legal counsel; pricing or consulting education.

## Competitor / Substitute Landscape

Bonsai, HoneyBook, and Dubsado combine proposals, contracts, invoices, payments, and client workflows. PandaDoc provides document templates, custom quotes, agreements, approvals, tracking, and payment collection. These products enable a scope-control process; they do not guarantee the consultant will set boundaries or enforce them.

## Pricing

**FACT:** Bonsai lists US$9–49/user/month annual-billed across public plans; HoneyBook starts at US$29/month annual-billed; Dubsado lists US$35/month or US$335/year for Starter and US$55/month or US$525/year for Premier; PandaDoc lists US$19/user/month annual-billed for Starter and US$49/user/month for Business. See [Bonsai](https://www.hellobonsai.com/pricing), [HoneyBook](https://www.honeybook.com/pricing), [Dubsado](https://www.dubsado.com/pricing), and [PandaDoc](https://www.pandadoc.com/pricing/). **UNKNOWN:** payment for a distinct scope-control layer, template, coach, or service for the target persona.

## Existing Spend

**MODERATE category spend:** linked observations show paid-oriented suite usage, and public tool pricing establishes purchasable process-enablement. **WEAK problem-specific spend:** no source shows an established solo consultant paying specifically to prevent scope leakage.

## Active Demand

**MODERATE:** direct advice-seeking on preventing scope creep and pricing/scope questions exists in the Scout corpus. This is not a search-volume measure and contains limited income-confirmed evidence.

## Switching Behavior

**WEAK:** suites offer migrations, templates, and consolidation, but the observations do not show established consultants switching specifically because scope control failed. The central switch may be behavioral—using a clearer process—not vendor replacement.

## Economic Impact

**MODERATE, unquantified:** OBS-000067 reports US$2,300 in claimed unpaid work. The six-figure retainer record establishes commercial stakes, but no frequency, typical effective-rate loss, or client-churn amount is verified.

## Current Solution Adequacy

**MEDIUM:** templates, deposits, revision limits, change orders, and agreements can solve much of the job if practiced consistently. Integrated suites provide supporting documents, reminders, and approval workflow. Residual failure may be consultant/client communication or discipline rather than missing capability.

## Process vs Tool Analysis

**PROCESS-LED.** The market offers tools for proposals, approvals, templates, and signatures. Evidence attributes leakage to informal scope handling and insufficiently explicit approvals, not an inability to create documents. A distinct product claim would require proof that configured tools/processes fail to surface or control changes.

## Market Structure

**FRAGMENTED / MIXED:** low-cost templates and process education coexist with document tools and integrated client suites. No clear standalone scope-control market is established for $100k+ solo consultants.

## Evidence For Commercial Importance

- A stated US$2,300 scope-loss example (OBS-000067).
- A just-over-six-figure retainer where scope/rate renegotiation recurs (OBS-000070).
- Published tools and paid plans directly supporting proposals, agreements, approvals, and payment terms.

## Evidence Against Commercial Importance

- Clear written scope, deposits, revision limits, and change approvals are established process controls.
- Existing tools already offer templates, agreements, and approval workflows.
- No direct target-persona payment for a distinct scope-control solution or prevalence measure.

## Key Unknowns

How often do income-confirmed $100k+ solo consultants lose money after they have a competent proposal/contract/change process, and would they pay beyond existing software or templates to prevent it?

## Market Analyst Assessment

Existing spend: **MODERATE** (software category; weak problem-specific spend). Active demand: **MODERATE**. Switching evidence: **WEAK**. Revenue/margin impact: **MODERATE**. Current solution satisfaction: **MEDIUM**. Recurring need: **MEDIUM**. Persona fit: **MEDIUM**. Process dependence: **HIGH**. Commercial importance: **MEDIUM**. Confidence: **67**.
