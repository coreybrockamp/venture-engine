# PROB-0010 Market Analysis — Training-Progress Information Continuity

## Executive Summary

**ASSESSMENT:** Owners can experience repeated tracking and information-handoff friction during a long training process, and a direct tracking app exists. However, the evidence shows no separate payment for tracking, while spreadsheets, trainer templates, phone notes, and existing apps can be adequate. Tracking appears principally bundled into owner implementation or professional support rather than a demonstrated standalone paid job.

## Existing Customer Evidence

**FACT:** An owner described reconstructing session and incident history across voice memos, Google Sheets/forms, notebooks, and memory before sharing with a veterinarian/trainer (OBS-000088). Another reported US$150 weekly behaviorist work while unable to judge progress (OBS-000091). Tracking requests and app feature requests are also documented (OBS-000102, OBS-000110).

## Buyer

**FACT:** The owner is the likely user and buyer. **UNKNOWN:** whether an owner would pay separately for a tracker rather than use a spreadsheet, notes, a trainer worksheet, or a feature bundled with training support.

## Current Workflow

Owners remember or manually log walks, reactions, triggers, distance, and training sessions; review them informally; and summarize history to a trainer, veterinarian, walker, or household member. Existing options include spreadsheets, notes, templates, and a dedicated app.

## Solution Categories

Paper or phone notes; spreadsheets and forms; trainer worksheets; training apps; behavior-specific tracker apps; shared household records; professional intake/history forms.

## Competitor / Substitute Landscape

**Reacpaw (COMP-0019)** is a direct consumer substitute. Its App Store listing describes reactivity insights, maps, shared accounts, and later trainer-connection features. Manual spreadsheets and trainer-created templates remain low-cost substitutes; professional services also gather behavior history during intake.

## Pricing

**UNKNOWN:** no published separate Reacpaw purchase or subscription price was verified in reviewed official/App Store materials. **FACT:** tracking is often bundled inside paid care or training workflows, such as the US$650 Behavior Vet intake/history review and written report, but that is not standalone tracking pricing. **REPORTED SPEND:** US$150 weekly behavior support in OBS-000091 is payment for support, not for tracking.

## Existing Spend

**WEAK for the tracking job:** care and training spend is visible, but no observation or published product price establishes payment specifically for progress logging, behavior records, or information sharing.

## Repeat Spend

**WEAK:** recurring behavior-support sessions can create a repeated need for records, but the tracking function itself has no verified subscription, retention, or repeat-payment evidence. Reacpaw’s current paid model is **UNKNOWN**.

## Active Demand

**MODERATE:** owners ask how to track, share, and interpret progress (OBS-000088, OBS-000091, OBS-000102). This is meaningful workflow interest, not evidence of a paid-intent market or search volume.

## Access / Availability

Low-cost recording options are broadly available. The relevant issue is adherence and usefulness under real daily handling conditions—not access scarcity. An owner noted that logging can be difficult to sustain, and an app reviewer requested changes to improve completeness (OBS-000102, OBS-000110).

## Trust / Selection

Owners need to decide what to record and how to interpret it, but the data does not show that they seek or purchase a separate trusted tracking intermediary. Interpretation may remain connected to an owner’s existing professional-support relationship.

## Current Solution Satisfaction

**MEDIUM:** a trainer-provided spreadsheet reportedly helped an owner see progress (OBS-000092), and Reacpaw reviewers report useful week-to-week visibility (OBS-000110). At the same time, owners describe fragmented logs, forgotten details, low tracking adherence, and requested app functionality (OBS-000088, OBS-000102, OBS-000110).

## Economic / Lifestyle Consequence

Tracking uncertainty can contribute to time burden and uncertainty during an already-paid support process. No direct time total, avoided cost, or incremental financial outcome is verified for the tracking function.

## Severe-Case Segmentation

Tracking intensity likely rises for owners in prolonged, highly engaged support processes. The corpus is support-seeking and self-selected, so it does not establish that ordinary or lower-need reactive-dog owners require a dedicated tracker.

## Market Structure

**EMERGING / SOFTWARE-LED but low-cost substitute-heavy.** A dedicated app exists, but the information task is also addressed by generic tools and professional workflows. There is no demonstrated standalone paid tracking category in the public evidence reviewed.

## Residual Paid Job

**NO DISTINCT RESIDUAL PAID JOB.** The potential residual is low-friction, sustained recordkeeping and sharing, but current evidence does not show that this is insufficiently solved by spreadsheets, templates, and Reacpaw or that owners pay separately to solve it. Whether tracking changes an owner decision or professional interaction remains unknown.

## Evidence For Commercial Importance

- Multiple owner accounts describe memory, comparison, and sharing friction.
- A direct tracker exists and has continuing feature development.
- A weekly paid-support record shows the information problem can coexist with ongoing spend.

## Evidence Against Commercial Importance

- A trainer spreadsheet was reported helpful.
- An existing app is useful for some owners.
- No separate price, paid-intent, retention, or outcome evidence for tracking exists.
- Logging adherence may be the root barrier rather than missing product capability.

## Key Unknowns

- Reacpaw’s current monetization, active use, and retention.
- How frequently owners abandon spreadsheets or tracking apps.
- Whether records materially improve owner decisions or professional coordination.
- Whether any owner pays specifically for shared training information rather than for training itself.

## Market Analyst Assessment

Existing spend: **WEAK**. Active demand: **MODERATE**. Repeat spend: **WEAK**. Failed-solution evidence: **WEAK**. Access friction: **WEAK**. Current solution satisfaction: **MEDIUM**. Buyer clarity: **HIGH**. Recurring need: **MEDIUM**. Commercial importance: **LOW**. Confidence: **68**. Residual paid job: **NONE**.
