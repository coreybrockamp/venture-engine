# PROB-0011 Market Analysis — Navigating Professional Behavior-Support Access and Fit

## Executive Summary

**ASSESSMENT:** Owners demonstrably spend on private training, classes, courses, and higher-cost specialty consultation, and they actively seek help. The market is fragmented across credential directories, local services, remote programs, and self-guided content. Existing directories reduce basic discovery friction, while local availability, provider fit, and case-specific suitability remain difficult to standardize. No evidence establishes that owners pay separately for navigation or matching after using existing directories, referrals, and provider intake.

## Existing Customer Evidence

**FACT:** Scout observations include a US$60 consultation perceived as low value (OBS-000089), a US$1,600 seven-hour quote (OBS-000090), US$900+ spent on a course reported unhelpful (OBS-000093), US$1,100–2,400 program evaluation (OBS-000096), and a US$1,200 lesson package reported helpful (OBS-000097). These are individual reports, not market rates.

## Buyer

**FACT:** The owner is the apparent user, decision-maker, and payer. **UNKNOWN:** whether the owner would pay separately for discovery, comparison, navigation, or matching rather than paying a provider directly.

## Current Workflow

Owners seek advice, compare self-training and paid help, review credentials or recommendations, compare price/availability, book a class or consultation, then continue training work or seek another option after disappointment or changed needs. The workflow can include an escalation from a trainer/course to specialty care, but the evidence does not establish a standard path.

## Solution Categories

Credential and professional directories; local private trainers and group classes; specialty behavior practices; virtual support; self-paced courses; subscriptions; peer recommendations; veterinarian referrals.

## Competitor / Substitute Landscape

- **IAABC Consultant Locator (COMP-0016):** location, dog-specialization, and virtual-consultation filtering for certified consultants.
- **Fear Free Directory (COMP-0017):** certified veterinary practices and multiple pet-professional roles.
- **SpiritDog Training (COMP-0018):** self-paced reactivity course and ongoing subscription content/support.
- **The Behavior Vet (COMP-0020):** appointment-based specialty service illustrating higher-cost, in-person-first care and follow-up.

**FACT:** directories offer discovery and credential signals; they do not publish local price, appointment availability, or fit/outcome information. Local services, courses, and specialty care create overlapping but non-equivalent alternatives.

## Pricing

**FACT — published local examples, not national rates:** The Behavior Vet lists a US$650 non-refundable initial 60–90-minute consultation in Connecticut; [Peach on a Leash](https://peachonaleash.com/dog-training-classes/) lists US$299 for five in-person weeks and US$59 for four online weeks; [Mohawk Hudson Humane Society](https://www.mohawkhumane.org/dog-training) lists a six-week class at US$240, including a currently full date; [Marin Humane](https://training.marinhumane.org/oh-behave/dogs/personalized-training-consultations/details/106-RD-Reactive-Dog-Get-Started-Package-15627) lists a three-session reactive-dog package at US$320.

**FACT — digital pricing:** SpiritDog’s official page lists a US$49 limited-time reactivity course and its academy lists US$47/month or US$470/year. These are vendor-published prices subject to change.

**REPORTED SPEND:** See OBS-000089, OBS-000090, OBS-000093, OBS-000096, and OBS-000097. **UNKNOWN:** a defensible national average, typical total cost, and the price of standalone matching/navigation.

## Existing Spend

**MODERATE:** direct owner payments, substantial program quotes, courses, and specialty-practice pricing show category spend. This does not show payment for an additional discovery or matching layer.

## Repeat Spend

**MODERATE:** OBS-000091 reports weekly behaviorist support; OBS-000094 reports continued trainer spending during a plateau; SpiritDog offers a subscription; and The Behavior Vet states follow-up is typically necessary. Frequency, renewal, and retention across the wider market remain **UNKNOWN**.

## Active Demand

**STRONG:** the Scout corpus contains repeated requests for trainer/class guidance, affordability comparisons, local options, and help after dissatisfaction (OBS-000090, OBS-000093, OBS-000096, OBS-000103, OBS-000105, OBS-000107, OBS-000108). This is owner-level solution seeking, not query-volume data.

## Access / Availability

**MODERATE:** IAABC, CCPDT, AVSAB, and Fear Free provide searchable credential/discovery paths, and IAABC supports virtual-consultation filtering. Public local listings also show limited class capacity, including a full Mohawk Hudson class and an owner-reported full class (OBS-000105). The Behavior Vet illustrates that initial specialty visits can be in-person and location-constrained, with later virtual follow-up subject to legal and established-patient requirements. This supports local availability friction, but does not measure a national specialist shortage.

## Trust / Selection

**MEDIUM friction:** multiple directories and credential systems offer selection aids, but owners still face provider-price, availability, and fit decisions. The evidence documents an unsatisfying paid consultation and uncertainty about trustworthy local help (OBS-000089, OBS-000107). It does not prove that directories fail or that an independent evaluator can determine appropriate care.

## Current Solution Satisfaction

**MEDIUM:** Some owners report paid lessons, classes, courses, and low-cost material as helpful (OBS-000097, OBS-000104, OBS-000106, OBS-000112). Others report low value, a failed course, or a plateau (OBS-000089, OBS-000093–000094). These records cannot establish typical satisfaction or efficacy.

## Economic / Lifestyle Consequence

Individual prices can be substantial, and prolonged support can recur. The Scout also records travel/care constraints, but that theme was not promoted because it is episodic and high-severity concentrated. No representative total owner cost or work-loss figure is established.

## Severe-Case Segmentation

**FACT:** Higher-need owners may face more repeated support, specialized care, travel, and provider-fit constraints. Less intensive cases can use local group classes, lower-priced online programs, or self-guided material. This is a customer-segmentation observation, not a clinical classification or recommendation.

## Market Structure

**FRAGMENTED / SERVICE-LED and INFORMATION/DIRECTORY-LED.** Local providers, specialty services, credential directories, and remote products overlap without one universal workflow. Provider capacity and owner/provider fit may be more decisive than a discovery-interface gap.

## Residual Paid Job

**NO DISTINCT RESIDUAL PAID JOB.** After an owner uses existing directories, referrals, local providers, virtual options, and course markets, the remaining issue appears to be actual provider availability and case-specific fit. The current record does not show owners paying a separate matching/navigation fee or that a generalized matching service can resolve capacity or fit.

## Evidence For Commercial Importance

- Direct owner payments and substantial quotes across multiple independent sources.
- Active comparison and help-seeking behavior.
- Published packages, classes, subscriptions, and specialty pricing demonstrate an active market.
- Ongoing support is explicitly visible in individual records and a specialty-practice workflow.

## Evidence Against Commercial Importance

- Multiple directories already support discovery and credential filtering.
- Local classes, remote training, and self-guided programs provide materially different price points.
- Provider fit and supply may require local or professional judgment rather than a product layer.
- No evidence supports separate navigation/matching willingness to pay.

## Key Unknowns

- Typical geography-specific availability, booking delay, and provider capacity.
- How often owners switch providers and why.
- Whether owners pay any third party specifically to choose or coordinate behavior support.
- Repeat spend and outcomes across severity levels.

## Market Analyst Assessment

Existing spend: **MODERATE**. Active demand: **STRONG**. Repeat spend: **MODERATE**. Failed-solution evidence: **MODERATE**. Access friction: **MODERATE**. Current solution satisfaction: **MEDIUM**. Buyer clarity: **HIGH**. Recurring need: **MEDIUM**. Commercial importance: **MEDIUM**. Confidence: **72**. Residual paid job: **NONE**.
