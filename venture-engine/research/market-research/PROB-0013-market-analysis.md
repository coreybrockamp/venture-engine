# Problem

**PROB-0013 — Household financial-document handoff.** This analysis covers client-side organization and transfer of records, not tax, legal, accounting, or investment advice.

## Executive Summary

The evidence supports real annual and event-driven collection work before a professional handoff. However, modern accounting-firm portals already provide document requests, checklists, organizers, secure upload, reminders, messaging, e-signature, and billing within the professional workflow. CPA fees evidence payment for professional service, not a separately purchased household handoff layer.

## Persona Fit

**MEDIUM.** One observation explicitly fits high-income/complex financial context; three are reasonable complexity proxies. The target persona’s prevalence and repeat frequency remain unknown.

## Buyer

The household operator prepares records; the CPA/accounting firm usually selects and pays for its portal. A household could buy a DMM, but no direct evidence shows it buys one for this narrow job.

## Current Workflow

Collect statements and forms, categorize transactions or P&Ls, refer to prior returns, maintain a spreadsheet/inventory, upload requested material, then answer follow-up questions.

## Existing Solution Categories

CPA/advisor portals and tax organizers; cloud folders; prior-return archives; checklists; shared spreadsheets; password managers; DMMs; and direct professional service.

## Competitor / Substitute Landscape

TaxDome is a representative firm-side portal: firms can request documents and information through a secure portal, organizer, checklist, mobile upload, reminders, signatures, messaging, and payment. DMMs are a human alternative for bill pay, record maintenance, and document organization. These replace or absorb portions of the household’s assembly work, though they do not guarantee client completeness.

## Pricing

TaxDome prices are firm-side and were not used as household pricing. Scout observations report approximately US$1,500 and US$3,500 in CPA fees after preparation; these are individual cases, not market rates. Reviewed DMM provider pricing is unpublished.

## Software Spend

**WEAK for the residual household job.** Portal functionality is normally bundled into an accounting engagement; no target household paid separately for a document-handoff tool.

## Human-Service Spend

**MODERATE category evidence.** CPA fees and DMM services show professional administrative spend. They do not establish spending specifically to collect or verify documents before an engagement.

## Active Demand

**WEAK.** Observations describe work and a paid professional relationship rather than direct search for a standalone handoff tool.

## Recurrence

**LOW to MEDIUM.** Tax preparation is generally annual; requests can recur through an engagement or life event. The repository does not measure recurrence across the persona.

## Life-Event Triggers

Tax season, a CPA/advisor request, a missing document, business/rental complexity, a household handoff, and a new professional relationship.

## Current Solution Adequacy

**MEDIUM to HIGH.** Portals and organizers can centralize requested uploads and reminders, while prior returns, folders, and checklists can be adequate. The remaining task is client response and record location—an implementation/behavior issue that may not warrant another tool.

## Tool Fragmentation

Documents originate across banks, employers, brokerages, insurers, passwords, folders, and professional portals. A portal reduces firm-facing fragmentation but does not become a household’s comprehensive financial system of record.

## Privacy / Trust

**HIGH constraint.** Hand-off includes sensitive documents and account information. The reviewed portal promotes two-factor authentication, secure messaging, and documents; introducing another intermediary would need a clear trust and access case.

## Economic / Time Consequence

One respondent reported six to ten hours and mental effort before CPA work; another described a missing-form lesson. These establish incidents, not a representative cost or incremental ROI.

## Market Structure

The accounting firm owns the workflow and chooses the client portal; clients use its requested checklist and upload process. DMMs and professional preparation services can absorb administration. This makes a direct-to-household wedge structurally difficult.

## Residual Paid Job

**NONE ESTABLISHED.** The residual—locating and confirming documents—exists, but payment is evidenced for CPA/DMM categories, not for a separate household product.

## Evidence For Commercial Importance

- Two reported CPA payments coexist with manual pre-handoff preparation.
- One reported six-to-ten-hour preparation burden.
- Firm portals invest in organizers, checklists, reminders, and secure documents, indicating the workflow matters to professional firms.

## Evidence Against Commercial Importance

- The professional portal can already structure collection within the engagement.
- No direct search or standalone payment evidence exists.
- Prior returns, folders, inventories, and simple workflows can be adequate.
- CPA category spend cannot be attributed to residual document assembly.

## Key Unknowns

How often portal workflows still fail for affluent complex households; whether they pay for DMM help; whether a firm would adopt a separate client-side system; measurable time/cost after an organizer is used; and which documents are safely shareable.

## Market Analyst Assessment

Commercial importance is **LOW to MEDIUM** as a professional-workflow component and **LOW** as a standalone consumer job. Confidence is **67/100**: the incumbent workflow is clear, but client-side residual pain and willingness to pay are not. No Opportunity should be generated from this record without direct evidence that existing portals and process support fail to resolve a paid, recurring job.
