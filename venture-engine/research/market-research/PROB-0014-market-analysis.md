# Market Analysis — PROB-0014: One-on-One Commitment and Follow-Through

**Persona:** PER-008 — New manager leading people for the first time  
**Stage:** Market Analysis only  
**Conclusion:** **NO — the problem is real, but a distinct residual paid job is not supported.**

## Scope and evidence standard

**FACT:** The analysis reviewed PROB-0014, its eight linked Scout observations, existing substitutes, and current first-party product/pricing materials. It created four competitor records and updated only PROB-0014’s market fields. It created no Opportunity, score, experiment, offer, landing page, or automation.

The question is not whether managers benefit from good 1:1s. It is whether an identifiable buyer plausibly pays separately for a residual commitment/context job after credible existing software, free workflows, coaching, and competent management practice are used.

## Incumbent adequacy and residual-job test

**FACT:** The core mechanical workflow in PROB-0014—retain context, document decisions, assign actions, surface incomplete actions, and prepare for recurring 1:1s—is covered by dedicated manager tools, HR suites, meeting software, AI assistants, and basic shared-document/task workflows.

**FACT:** The Scout corpus itself provides adequacy evidence: shared documents and templates supported focused follow-up (OBS-000141); routine cadence was described as sufficient for some teams (OBS-000143); 15Five was described as useful for newer managers (OBS-000155); and a shared spreadsheet worked for promotion/performance clarity (OBS-000159).

**FACT:** The clearest residual-failure evidence is a missed development commitment (OBS-000138). The only incumbent-specific failure, OBS-000156, is one App Store review about priority carry-forward/retyping and has low-to-medium first-time-manager fit. Neither shows that a manager who already uses a competent tool/process still has a repeated, consequential, cross-system failure for which they seek or pay for another layer.

**INFERENCE:** What remains after competent adoption is primarily operating discipline, conversational judgment, organizational enablement, and integration/configuration quality—not a clearly bounded unmet product job. This inference is constrained by a small, nonrepresentative Scout sample.

## Willingness to pay and buyer/payer analysis

### Category spend

**FACT:** There is company-level spend on people/performance software: Lattice publishes per-seat prices and a US$4,000 annual minimum; 15Five publishes paid AI add-ons. Repository observations also report named-tool use (OBS-000147, OBS-000155–000156) and employer-provided training/coaching (OBS-000146).

### Specific residual-job willingness to pay

**UNKNOWN:** No source shows a manager, People/HR team, or employer paying separately for an additional layer after using shared docs, task tracking, a manager platform, AI meeting assistance, coaching, or mentoring. No switching, procurement, retention, or paid workaround evidence identifies a residual follow-through product job.

### Actor map

| Actor | Evidence-backed role | What remains unknown |
|---|---|---|
| First-time manager | End user experiencing missed context or manual follow-up work. | Whether they have budget authority or will buy a separate tool. |
| Direct report | Collaborator and affected party in shared agendas/actions. | Whether they influence tool selection or would adopt another system. |
| HR / People Operations / department | Likely selector/payer for Lattice and qualifying 15Five packages, based on their B2B pricing and organizational workflows. | Whether they perceive an unbundled follow-through layer as a separate procurement need. |
| L&D / senior manager / coach | Often supplies mentorship, training, or coaching. | Whether it pays for or needs a standalone operating-workflow product. |

**INFERENCE:** The economic buyer is more likely organizational than an individual new manager, but the evidence does not show a buyer gap that existing suites and manager-development programs leave open.

## Frequency, consequence, and evidence quality

- **Frequency:** 1:1s and follow-up can recur weekly, fortnightly, or periodically. Exact prevalence, duration, and persistence after the earliest manager transition are unknown.
- **Consequence:** One missed commitment lasted about a month (OBS-000138), and one source reports manual notes/preparation (OBS-000147). No representative retention, performance, legal, or economic consequence is established.
- **Source quality:** The originating Problem has 8 observations, 7 URLs, and 3 source types, but Reddit supplies five observations. Hacker News is technology-adjacent; the App Store input is self-selected; direct first-time-manager fit is uneven outside Reddit.
- **Market-source diversity:** Incumbent coverage comes from four current first-party product/documentation sources plus the repository’s independent community/review observations. Vendor materials demonstrate claimed capability and pricing, not typical customer outcomes.

## Conclusion and confidence

**Conclusion:** **NO — problem real, but incumbents and competent process adequately absorb the documented mechanics; no distinct residual paid job is supported.**

**Confidence in conclusion:** **MEDIUM (70/100 analytical confidence; not an opportunity score).** The conclusion is strengthened by direct feature overlap across manager, HR, meeting, AI, and free-process substitutes plus repository adequacy evidence. It is limited by weak buyer/payer evidence, lack of post-adoption failure cases, and a small Scout sample.

**Do not infer:** Category pricing, employer L&D budgets, or a user complaint about a particular tool proves willingness to pay for an additional product.

## Residual evidence needed before reconsideration

1. Multiple first-time managers at independent organizations who use competent 1:1/task/HR tooling yet repeatedly lose consequential commitments or duplicate work across systems.
2. A bounded failure that is not resolved by training, coaching, clear process, or configuration.
3. Evidence that an identifiable organizational buyer seeks, switches to, or pays for an unbundled solution specifically for that residual workflow.
