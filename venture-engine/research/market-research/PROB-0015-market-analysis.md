# Market Analysis — PROB-0015: Rehab-to-Training Workflow Coordination

**Persona:** PER-002 — Recreational athlete dealing with a chronic or nagging injury
**Stage:** Market Analysis only
**Conclusion:** **NO — the workflow is real, but a distinct residual paid job is not supported.**

## Scope and evidence standard

**FACT:** This analysis reviewed PROB-0015, its eight linked Scout observations, three current first-party substitute records, and relevant source documentation. It created COMP-0030 through COMP-0032 and updated only PROB-0015’s market fields. It created no Opportunity, score, experiment, offer, landing page, or automation.

The question is not whether recovery and return-to-sport are difficult. It is whether an identifiable buyer plausibly pays separately for a **non-clinical** coordination workflow after competent care, coaching, software, and ordinary communication/process are available. Diagnosis, treatment, clearance, symptom interpretation, exercise prescription, and coaching judgment are excluded.

## Cross-system coordination and residual-job test

**FACT:** The alternatives cover the principal component workflows: clinician-to-athlete program delivery and updates (Physitrack), care-team/digital-to-in-person coordination (HingeConnect), and coach-athlete plan/data/messages (TrainingPeaks). These are supplemented by clinician or coach communication and ordinary shared records.

**FACT:** The strongest post-adoption operational failure remains OBS-000160: a runner with paid coach and physio support received incompatible return plans and had to reconcile them after the providers did not coordinate. OBS-000168 is a single adaptive-training-app adjustment report. OBS-000173 and OBS-000175 are two narrow Exakt-review reports concerning delayed symptom logging/progression, but they are regional listings for the same product.

**INFERENCE:** This is insufficient to show a recurring, tool-independent coordination job that survives competent care and coaching. The evidence can also reflect provider communication quality, app configuration, care access, or normal coaching decisions. None proves that the athlete repeatedly performs a bounded operational task that current products and process cannot absorb.

## Paid workaround, willingness to pay, and buyer/payer analysis

### Category spend

**FACT:** The evidence shows existing spend or covered access: paid coach-supported training (OBS-000164), paid plans/apps (OBS-000166), remote post-PT training (OBS-000169), insurance-backed care (OBS-000167, OBS-000176), and paid recovery services (OBS-000178–000179). Physitrack has a practitioner buyer, TrainingPeaks has coach/athlete subscription paths, and Hinge Health is positioned through employers/health plans.

### Coordination-specific spend

**NOT FOUND:** No source shows an athlete, clinician, coach, practice, employer, or insurer paying specifically to synchronize already-given rehab constraints with an otherwise competent training workflow. OBS-000164 and OBS-000169 are paid human-support examples that the users reported as adequate, not evidence of a separately purchased bridge.

### Actor map

| Actor | Evidence-backed role | Remaining uncertainty |
|---|---|---|
| Athlete | End user who may reconcile guidance, track activity, or communicate updates. | No evidence of discretionary budget or payment for a distinct coordination layer. |
| Coach / trainer | Can manage training plan, data, comments, and communication; may bundle TrainingPeaks Premium into fees. | No evidence that coaches pay for an additional rehab-handoff tool after ordinary client communication. |

**INFERENCE:** The actors who can purchase established products are fragmented, and none is evidenced as an economic buyer for a standalone non-clinical handoff layer.

## Incumbent adequacy, frequency, and consequence

**FACT:** Positive adequacy evidence is substantial: paid coach feedback helped rebuild consistency (OBS-000164); staged clinician-led progression worked (OBS-000167); remote trainer support worked (OBS-000169); Recover Athletics, PhysiApp, and Hinge Health were reported useful (OBS-000172, OBS-000174, OBS-000176); and paid recovery services were positively reviewed (OBS-000178–000179).

**FACT:** The evidence does not quantify how often cross-provider conflict repeats, how long an athlete spends transferring information, whether it causes financial loss, or whether it persists once the athlete has a competent clinician, coach, and communication routine. There is no demonstrated paid case-management or concierge workaround for the specific operational job.

## Evidence quality and safety boundary

- **Source diversity:** The originating Problem uses eight URLs across Reddit, public forums, and App Store reviews; Reddit supplies four observations. The market map adds three current first-party vendor sources. Vendor documentation establishes features and commercial models, not customer outcomes or prevalence.
- **Persona fit:** Six supporting records are high fit and two medium fit, but product reviews and communities remain self-selected. Hinge Health is a meaningful substitute but does not establish that all recreational athletes have access.
- **Uncertainty:** Buyer, payer, frequency, prevalence, time cost, economic consequence, and post-competent-adoption persistence are unknown.
- **Safety:** This analysis does not diagnose, interpret symptoms, prescribe treatment/exercise, assess provider quality, or determine clearance. It assesses only market/workflow coverage.

## Conclusion and confidence

**Conclusion:** **NO — the workflow is real, but clinicians, coaches, integrated/digital care, training platforms, and competent manual communication/process adequately absorb the documented component work. No distinct residual paid job is supported.**

**Confidence in conclusion:** **MEDIUM (72/100 analytical confidence; not an opportunity score).** It is strengthened by direct component coverage and multiple adequacy accounts. It remains limited by a small, self-selected Scout corpus and the possibility that a future independent, recurring cross-provider failure with a proven buyer could differ.

## Residual evidence needed before reconsideration

1. Multiple independent athletes using competent clinician, coach, and established software/process who repeatedly perform the same consequential coordination task.
2. Evidence that the task is operational—not clinical judgment, ordinary coaching, adherence, or a one-product defect—and cannot be resolved through normal provider communication or configuration.
3. A buyer/selector who seeks, switches to, or pays specifically for the residual coordination work.
