# PER-003 Opportunity Generation

## Scope

**FACT:** This Opportunity Generator pass considered the three PER-003 researching problems, then created three unscored commercial hypotheses. No scoring, strategic decision, experiment, landing page, automation, or new research observation was created.

Each hypothesis has substantial contrary evidence: incumbents cover core workflows, some users report positive outcomes, and separate willingness to pay is unknown.

## Residual-Pain Classification

PROB-0005 is **B** only for the narrow disruption/exception-decision hypothesis: current suites schedule and dispatch, but OBS-000063 and OBS-000065 show manual ordering or a field-information gap. PROB-0006 is **B** only for a human-intervention queue: suites provide reminders and quote-to-cash workflows, but records show backlog and rework. PROB-0007 is **C**: public evidence is most consistent with selection, setup, migration, and adoption friction rather than an unserved FSM tier.

## Rejected Opportunity Directions

- **Another full FSM:** rejected because all five reviewed incumbents already combine scheduling, dispatch, CRM, quotes, invoicing, payments, and mobile workflows; replacement switching cost is high.
- **A basic scheduling app:** rejected because it does not identify a residual task beyond incumbent scheduling; the supported hypothesis is limited to disruption decisions.
- **Generic CRM or basic invoice automation:** rejected because incumbents already cover these functions, including Jobber automated quote/invoice follow-up.
- **“ServiceTitan for smaller businesses”:** rejected because published team tiers and product segmentation undermine the premise; the market evidence cannot establish a structural missing tier.

## Generator Critique

The upstream records provide clear category spend, recurrent workflows, and narrow examples of implementation or exception friction. The difficult distinction is that all three problem areas are core FSM territory: isolated complaints may reflect configuration, mobile defects, or normal segmentation rather than a separate market. The two add-on hypotheses are only compelling if they make a decision that existing dashboards, automation, and dispatcher labor leave unresolved. The Scorer should be especially skeptical of incremental willingness to pay, frequency after competent adoption, integration access, and any claim of measured economic impact.

## Next Boundary

The records are researching hypotheses only. A future authorized scoring pass must not treat incumbent coverage or Tier 4 problem evidence as proof of demand for another subscription or service.
