## Residual-Pain Assessment

### PROB-0012 — Household Financial-Information Continuity

**Classification: D — ALREADY SOLVED for a general product; privacy-bounded residual workflow is UNKNOWN.**

**FACT:** The observations document real account fragmentation, continuity concerns, manual inventory work, and active configuration seeking (OBS-000113, OBS-000115, OBS-000117–000119, OBS-000123–000126, OBS-000131–000135). However, paid aggregation and collaboration tools already cover the shared-view component: one household reported paid shared aggregation (OBS-000114), another reported a shared app that retained account ownership while giving both people a full picture (OBS-000125), and an App Store reviewer reported the same household-view workflow was adequate (OBS-000128). Monarch, Quicken Simplifi, 1Password Families, folders, consolidation, autopay, periodic review, and daily money managers materially cover aggregation, secure sharing, organization, or delegated administration (COMP-0021, COMP-0022, COMP-0023, COMP-0025).

**FACT:** Product-specific data failures remain visible in individual reviews (OBS-000127, OBS-000129, OBS-000130, OBS-000136, OBS-000137), but they do not establish a cross-product, target-persona residual job or payment for a replacement layer. Full-account sharing can also be an intentional privacy boundary rather than a product defect.

**UNKNOWN:** whether high-income households with a competent finance app, password manager, and documented review routine still face a repeated, decision-relevant continuity failure that they would pay separately to resolve. No observation establishes a distinct buyer, price, purchase trigger, retention, or economic outcome for such a layer. A generic household finance dashboard, shared account inventory, credential/document vault, continuity checklist subscription, aggregation-reliability layer, or setup/optimization service is therefore not promoted.

### PROB-0013 — Household Financial-Document Handoff

**Classification: C — PROCESS/SERVICE-DEPENDENT.**

**FACT:** The observations show pre-CPA record assembly, one reported six-to-ten-hour preparation burden, prior-return/folder workflows, and reported CPA spending (OBS-000121, OBS-000122, OBS-000132, OBS-000134). The reported payments are for CPA work, not a separate client-side document-assembly product. TaxDome’s firm-side portal supplies document requests, organizers, checklists, reminders, secure exchange, signatures, and payment; DMM services can absorb records and document administration (COMP-0024, COMP-0025).

**FACT:** Existing returns, shared folders, checklists, and simpler account arrangements can be adequate (OBS-000132, OBS-000134). The firm normally owns portal choice and the client still must locate/respond to requested records.

**UNKNOWN:** whether a household repeatedly pays for a standalone pre-handoff workflow after using a competent professional process, portal, and existing recordkeeping tools. No direct search, buyer commitment, incremental economic outcome, or standalone payment is recorded. A generic tax-document organizer, financial-document vault, CPA-preparation checklist, portal add-on, upload concierge, or household DMM-matching service is therefore not promoted.

## Buyer, Willingness-to-Pay, and Differentiation Check

**FACT:** The household financial operator is the likely user for both Problems; the couple may be a joint buyer for continuity, while the accounting firm selects the professional portal for document handoff. This buyer mapping does not establish separate demand.

**FACT:** Finance-app subscriptions, reported CPA fees, and monthly-billed DMM services validate adjacent category spend only. They do not demonstrate payment for a distinct continuity or handoff layer. **UNKNOWN:** a separate price, purchase trigger, retention signal, or buyer-level ROI for either residual job.

Both candidate areas fail the differentiation test. A lower-friction process, existing apps, shared/private credential vaults, folders, account simplification, firm portals, or human service are plausible alternatives to every proposed generic product or service direction. Existing tool failures are either individual/product-specific or not shown to persist after competent setup.

## Rejected Opportunity Directions

- **Shared household finance dashboard or another account-aggregation product:** rejected; paid incumbents already cover the core view and collaboration workflow.
- **Continuity run-book, account inventory, credential vault, or document-vault subscription:** rejected; folders, password managers, review routines, and simplification are plausible adequate alternatives; separate payment is unproven.
- **Aggregation-reliability overlay or reconciliation service:** rejected; available evidence is review-heavy and product/institution-specific, with no cross-product residual buyer or willingness to pay.
- **Generic CPA/tax-document organizer, checklist, or portal add-on:** rejected; accounting-firm portals and existing document processes cover core collection; paid CPA work is not payment for another layer.
- **Household document-upload concierge or DMM-matching service:** rejected; no evidence identifies a separately paid, repeatable client-side job after professional process or DMM availability.

## Evidence Needed Before Reconsideration

1. **PROB-0012:** independently sourced cases showing that households with competent finance apps, credential sharing, and a documented process still incur a repeatable continuity failure; measurable consequence; and payment or credible switching for a narrow, privacy-bounded remedy.
2. **PROB-0013:** independently sourced evidence that households pay separately to collect, verify, or hand off documents after using a firm portal or competent professional process; including frequency, buyer, price, and outcome.

Zero promotion is warranted.
