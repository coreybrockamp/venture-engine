## Zero-Opportunity Persona Lessons

### PER-004 — Solo Consultant

**FACT:** Scout evidence showed a US$2,300 scope-loss example, recurring administration, and paid-suite use. Market review showed that competent process, templates, contracts, Bonsai, HoneyBook, Dubsado, and PandaDoc absorb the general jobs. The residual was unproven after competent setup. **Lesson:** do not equate a painful process failure with a product gap; require a measurable failure that persists after normal controls.

### PER-006 — Reactive-Dog Owner

**FACT:** Scout evidence showed individual spend, active searches, disappointing paid options, and high lifestyle consequence. Market review showed that directories, local services, courses, training professionals, spreadsheets, and a dedicated tracking app absorb the broad jobs. Remaining access/fit limits depend on supply and professional judgment. **Lesson:** high emotional urgency and category spend can coexist with no safe, distinct, non-clinical product wedge.

## Scoring-System Review

**Verdict: REASONABLE.** The existing rubric separates category spend from opportunity-specific WTP: PER-001's best evidence-backed score is 59 and PER-003's is 43, despite strong paid categories. Incumbent coverage lowered poor-solution and WTP assessments; unclear buyers lowered confidence; and market-size evidence remained weak rather than being assumed. Founder fit stayed neutral at 5, as required by the evidence record.

No scored Opportunity looks obviously under-scored on current evidence. OPP-0001 and OPP-0004 are the closest calls, but each still lacks direct buyer payment for its narrower offer. The 70-point threshold prevented premature tests rather than blocking a clearly validated candidate. Four personas and eight scores are too small a sample to prove permanent calibration.

## Research-Engine Review

| Stage | Working well | Too loose / false-positive risk | Too conservative / false-negative risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| Scout | Atomic evidence, source diversity, and preserved counterevidence. | Emotional intensity, category spend, reviews, and support-seeking samples can overstate product demand. | Strict source quality can miss private or hard-to-observe paid workflows. |
| Problem Clusterer | Independent URL/source-type counts and explicit contradictions. | A repeated complaint can still be process- or service-led. | Narrow but valuable post-incumbent problems can be missed in broad clustering. |
| Market Analyst | Alternatives, pricing, and residual-job checks distinguish market activity from a gap. | Public vendor materials may overstate alternative coverage. | Public records can understate hidden workflow failures or switching cost. |
| Opportunity Generator | Zero-promotion is permitted and durable rejections are retained. | A plausible service wedge could be rejected if direct WTP is not public. | The residual-job standard may delay a narrow opportunity until targeted buyer evidence exists. |
| Opportunity Scorer | Fixed weights, neutral founder fit, score history, and a 70-point gate prevent optimism. | Low-confidence numeric estimates can imply precision. | Conservative WTP and market-size handling may compress early hypotheses below validation range. |

**ASSESSMENT:** The largest false-positive risk is treating category spending or an emotional complaint as payment for an add-on. The largest false-negative risk is under-observing narrow, post-incumbent workflows that require direct buyer access to reveal.

## Persona #5 Selection Framework

Ranked desired characteristics:

1. A clear self-buyer or economic buyer who can authorize payment for the exact job.
2. A high-frequency, recurring workflow with a measurable cash, time, or risk consequence.
3. Direct evidence of paid manual workarounds or an inadequate incumbent after competent use.
4. A narrow, identifiable trigger and reachable audience that permits a cheap, ethical test.
5. Moderate or low regulatory/trust burden and a credible switching path.
6. Fragmented workflow data that can be acted on without deep integrations or professional judgment.

Existing spend, a manual workaround, and repeat purchase potential remain valuable only when tied to the residual job rather than the broad category.

## Recommended Persona #5

**HYPOTHESIS: PER-009 — High-income household that feels financially disorganized.**

It is the best next learning target because the definition suggests a potentially clear household economic buyer, recurring shared administration, identifiable financial/administrative triggers, and measurable non-advice outcomes such as time, missed renewals, duplicated work, or fees. It tests a failure mode not fully tested so far: whether a high-capacity consumer household pays for a narrow shared-administration workflow after using existing tools and advisors.

The same attributes make it risky: consumer-finance tools and advisors may be strong substitutes, and financial trust boundaries are material. The Scout should prioritize first-person evidence of repeated manual reconciliation, current paid workaround/advisor/software use, incumbent abandonment after competent setup, a discrete non-advice job, and a concrete payment or switching signal. It should not infer demand from income, account complexity, or generic financial anxiety.
