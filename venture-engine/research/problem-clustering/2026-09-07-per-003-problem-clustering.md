## PROB-0005 - Crew schedule and field/office coordination

**Problem statement:** Local field-service owners struggle to keep crew schedules, job information, and field/office updates reliably coordinated as team and route complexity grows, creating recurring administrative work and operational risk.

**Owner impact:** Scheduling friction can consume office/owner time, reduce reliable crew deployment, and impair capacity planning. No source quantifies technician utilization or revenue lost.

**Evidence:** A 15-technician shop outgrew a shared calendar (OBS-000053); an eight-route company sought to replace QuickBooks across routes and tickets (OBS-000051); a co-owner still manually orders recurring routes (OBS-000063); and a master-plumber owner uses dispatch daily but reports incomplete mobile field visibility (OBS-000065).

**Contradiction:** Jobber and other paid systems are also reported to make operations more organized. The evidence supports a coordination risk during growth, not an assertion that dispatch software is broadly inadequate.

## PROB-0006 - Quote-to-cash handoff friction

**Problem statement:** Local field-service owners can lose sales velocity and delay cash collection when estimates, job documentation, invoicing, and payment follow-up move slowly or require manual office rework.

**Owner impact:** Delayed estimates can weaken sales follow-up; paper-to-office billing and invoice rework create administrative time and may delay collection. No verified dollar loss, conversion rate, or days-sales-outstanding measure exists.

**Evidence:** A contractor reported twelve estimates waiting during a busy period (OBS-000049), another sought bid-follow-up help when work declined (OBS-000050), and a Service Fusion user reported recurring desktop rework after mobile invoices failed to sync (OBS-000064).

**Contradiction:** An HVAC reviewer said integrated software reduced invoice chasing (OBS-000060). This may be an implementation or tool-selection issue rather than a persistent unmet need.

## PROB-0007 - Field-service systems-fit gap

**Problem statement:** Growing local service businesses face a systems-fit gap: simple tools become insufficient for connected field operations, while more comprehensive field-service systems can impose price, onboarding, feature, or switching burdens that constrain adoption.

**Owner impact:** The trade-off affects recurring software cost, implementation time, operational continuity, and the ability to scale without manual work or a disruptive migration.

**Evidence:** The cluster includes active comparisons (OBS-000044, OBS-000046, OBS-000047, OBS-000051), a seven-truck owner reporting rising renewal fees (OBS-000055), complex small-business onboarding (OBS-000058), and additional-user pricing prompting alternative consideration (OBS-000062).

**Contradiction:** Several reports say systems work well, reduce admin work, or scale with the business (OBS-000048, OBS-000055, OBS-000061). The problem is fit and adoption at a growth stage, not a blanket vendor-failure claim.

## Rejected, merged, and reframed themes

- **Software fragmentation** was not promoted verbatim. Its root manifestations were split between crew coordination (PROB-0005) and quote-to-cash handoffs (PROB-0006); some records were vendor-specific implementation issues.
- **Scheduling/dispatch** became PROB-0005 because multi-technician coordination and field visibility have a distinct owner capacity consequence.
- **Software cost/switching** became PROB-0007 because independent records describe the growth-stage fit trade-off, not merely a complaint about one vendor.
- **Estimating/follow-up** and **collections/cash flow** merged into PROB-0006 because both concern converting work/quotes into invoices and payment without delay.
- **Job costing/margin** was not promoted: one strong target-range observation (OBS-000048) and one adjacent review request (OBS-000063) are insufficient to establish a distinct repeated owner problem.
- **Owner admin capacity** was not promoted: OBS-000061 is stronger counterevidence that integrated software can avert an admin hire than proof of a recurring unmet need.

## Evidence gaps before any market analysis

- Independent current owner statements from explicitly 5-25 employee firms quantifying missed estimates, collections delay, utilization, or margin loss.
- Evidence separating poor implementation from persistent product-market gaps.
- Current, owner-level evidence on job costing, hiring/training, material coordination, and repeat-customer workflows.
- Details on which solutions owners have actually evaluated, bought, retained, or abandoned, and why.
