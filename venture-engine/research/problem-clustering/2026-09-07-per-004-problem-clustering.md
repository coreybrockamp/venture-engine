## PROB-0008 — Scope-Control and Effective-Rate Leakage

**Problem statement:** Established solo consultants can lose effective project profitability when changing requests, revisions, or deliverable changes are not converted into a revised scope, price, and approval before additional work is performed.

**Persona fit:** HIGH: OBS-000070 (explicit just-over-six-figure retainer). MEDIUM: OBS-000067 (US$100/hour project) and OBS-000071 (six steady clients). LOW/context: OBS-000068 and industry-community guidance OBS-000086/000087.

**Commercial signal:** Existing spend is **WEAK** for a distinct scope-control offer, though commercial work and high-rate pricing are directly visible. Revenue/margin impact is **MODERATE** because OBS-000067 reports US$2,300 in claimed unpaid work. Recurring labor burden is **MODERATE**; active solution seeking is **MODERATE**; current solution adequacy is **MEDIUM** because manual process controls are documented.

**Process vs tool:** **PROCESS-DRIVEN.** The evidence points to unclear commercial boundaries and weak change approval. Proposals, deposits, revision limits, and explicit paid change orders are the primary current controls; no record demonstrates that software absence is the root cause.

**Contradiction:** scope leakage may be avoidable through experienced boundary-setting, clear proposals, deposits, and written approval. The one quantified loss does not establish prevalence among $100k+ solo consultants.

## PROB-0009 — Client-to-Cash Administration Handoffs

**Problem statement:** Established solo consultants can spend non-billable effort reconstructing client, time, invoice, payment, and scheduling state when client-to-cash workflows rely on manual handoffs or partial integrations, which can delay billing or create booking and data-entry errors.

**Persona fit:** MEDIUM: OBS-000073 (six-figure-oriented freelance context), OBS-000076 (freelance consultant), OBS-000077 (consultation billing). LOW/context: OBS-000078 and OBS-000080 (review accounts with unclear scale). No observation is HIGH.

**Commercial signal:** Existing spend is **MODERATE** because Bonsai, HoneyBook, billing, and time-tracking use are visible. Revenue/margin impact and recurring labor burden are **MODERATE** but unquantified. Active solution seeking is **WEAK**. Current solution adequacy is **MEDIUM** because some documented workflows are lightweight and reliable.

**Process vs tool:** **MIXED.** CSV handoffs and calendar/payment defects show possible tool/integration limitations, while templates, simple billing processes, and disciplined workflow design can resolve the same work for others.

**Contradiction:** OBS-000074 reports a veteran freelancer spending roughly ten minutes per month invoicing with a template/script. OBS-000075, OBS-000079, and OBS-000082 show paid all-in-one or automated workflows working for some users. The evidence does not show that a new tool is necessary.

## Rejected, Merged, and Reframed Themes

- **Pricing/profitability** merged into PROB-0008 because the supported root consequence is effective-rate erosion when commercial scope changes; two observations do not establish a separate, repeatable project-profitability problem.
- **Collections/cash flow** merged into PROB-0009 only where it reflects the client-to-cash administrative handoff. A broad collections Problem was rejected: evidence includes industry guidance and isolated tool defects, while experienced freelancers report adequate templates/processes.
- **Tool fragmentation** was not promoted as its own Problem. Multiple paid and lightweight tools demonstrably work; isolated sync/customization complaints do not prove a durable market-wide gap.
- **Non-billable admin** was reframed into PROB-0009. The observations identify tasks but do not establish that normal business overhead is an independent high-severity Problem.
- **Pipeline/capacity** was rejected. OBS-000069 and OBS-000072 show active questions, but only two sources and no verified loss, utilization constraint, or mature-solo prevalence.

## Evidence Gaps for Any Future Market Analysis

- Direct, current owner-level evidence from income-confirmed $100k+ solo consultants on scope frequency, effective-rate loss, and paid remedies.
- Whether a process-first change-control approach is already adequate for most established consultants.
- Time and cash consequences of client-to-cash reconciliation after competent configuration of existing tools.
- Independent willingness to pay for any residual workflow beyond Bonsai, HoneyBook, templates, accounting tools, and manual operating discipline.
- Whether the identified patterns hold across consultant specializations rather than writers, developers, and creative professionals.
