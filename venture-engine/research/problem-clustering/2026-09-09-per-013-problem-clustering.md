# PER-013 Problem Clustering — Outpatient Referral-Operations Manager

## Scope and result

**FACT:** This pass reviewed all 21 canonical PER-013 Scout observations and created **zero Problems**. No Market Analysis, competitor, Opportunity, score, experiment, offer, landing page, or automation was created.

The corpus confirms that multi-site outpatient organizations use paid coordinator labor, worklists, calls, fax follow-up, scanning, and existing referral systems around external referrals. It does not establish that one narrow, recurring structural administrative loop-closure mechanism persists after competent adoption across materially different mature EHR/referral environments. The formal Problem-promotion bar is therefore not met.

## Structural-administrative assessment

**FACT:** Missing status and returned documentation across independent organizations are a recognizable administrative category. C-EMR participants describe manual reports follow-up; an Epic-based job description includes scanning outside records; and an Epic user reports re-entry of external referrals.

**ASSESSMENT:** A narrow structural administrative hypothesis exists, but it does not survive the formal bar. Cross-organizational recurrence is present at the category level; cross-platform recurrence is weaker because the adverse evidence is concentrated in C-EMR and Epic, while NextGen, Cerner, and ReferralMD primarily document process or incumbent coverage. Most importantly, the corpus does not show the same residual after competent mature adoption in two distinct stacks. No formal Problem is created.

## Human-work boundary

**FACT:** Daily worklists, coordinator calls, future follow-up flags, faxed requests, record scanning, and large referral-center staffing preserve evidence of paid-human bridging (OBS-000221–000222, OBS-000226–000227, OBS-000239).

**ASSESSMENT:** Only a portion is plausibly structural status/documentation bridging. Appointment confirmation may require patient outreach and choice; external-office contact may require clarification or relationship work; clinical triage and returned-finding interpretation are excluded; and payer/authorization queues are explicitly separate in the Epic material (OBS-000234). The corpus cannot allocate coordinator labor between these categories, so it must not treat total coordinator labor as a reducible administrative product gap.

## Incumbent and process adequacy

**FACT:** Visualutions improved referral tracking and clearing old orders (OBS-000223). UNC describes Epic/CareLink workqueues, reports, ownership, and completion tracking (OBS-000224–000225). UCSF combined Epic, OCR, a custom interface, patient outreach, and Luma Health while retaining exception review (OBS-000229–000230). NextGen describes return-report agreements (OBS-000231), UI Health Care describes Epic workqueues (OBS-000233), Care Everywhere coverage may address the reported Epic re-entry workflow (OBS-000236), Cerner reports material improvement from centralized Direct Referrals plus process/training (OBS-000238), and ReferralMD reports higher coordinator throughput with Epic integration and alerts (OBS-000240).

These accounts do not prove universal adequacy. They do show that competent configuration, interoperability, designated ownership, external-provider agreements, centralized process, and dedicated referral platforms can absorb material portions of the proposed mechanism.

## Excluded or unpromoted boundaries

- **Patient adherence/choice:** scheduling, no-shows, declines, and unreachability are operationally relevant but are not established as a system/interoperability Problem.
- **Payer/authorization work:** authorization and denial queues are distinct from referral-loop closure in OBS-000234 and are not merged into this result.
- **Clinical judgment:** triage, referral appropriateness, specialist selection, and interpretation of findings are outside the administrative hypothesis.
- **Relationship-sensitive coordination:** patient and external-office communication can be necessary human work, not product failure.
- **Implementation/configuration and product-specific friction:** the Epic manual-entry account may be addressable through configuration/add-on coverage; the OCR exception case is intake-specific; no product-specific limitation is generalized.

## Evidence-quality constraints

**FACT:** The Scout set contains 21 observations, 10 independent URLs, and 5 source types. The narrowest candidate has only 3 independent supporting URLs and three supporting source types. Multiple strongest C-EMR observations come from one URL, so they are not independent confirmation. Vendor/case-study material is concentrated in incumbent counterevidence and cannot substitute for operator evidence.

**UNKNOWN:** buyer authority, payer, separate willingness to pay, task-level labor allocation, cost, frequency, prevalence by organization, deployment maturity, external-referral mix, and residual burden after competent adoption. Public sources do not reliably separate structural bridging from patient, payer, clinical, and relationship tasks. No source establishes a commercial opportunity.

## CFD-originated mechanism check (non-canonical traceability)

**Survived:** paid coordinator labor, external status/documentation work, calls/fax/worklists, scanning/matching, and cross-organization workflow recurrence independently appeared in the Scout corpus.

**Weakened:** the CFD Path A structural-generalization result narrowed materially. The same detailed residual did not persist across two independent, competent mature stacks; NextGen, Cerner, ReferralMD, and much of Epic contribute coverage or process evidence rather than adverse residual evidence.

**Conclusion:** Coordinator labor is a mixture of structural bridging and necessary patient, payer, clinical, and relationship work. The v2 paid-human and cross-implementation signals were useful for locating a testable workflow, but they were insufficient predictors of a formal Problem. One case does not justify changing the CFD gate.
