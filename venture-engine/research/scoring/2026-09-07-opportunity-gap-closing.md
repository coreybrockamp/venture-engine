# Step 9 Opportunity Gap Closing - PER-001

## Scope and method

**FACT:** This pass rescored only OPP-0001, OPP-0004, and OPP-0002 using the unchanged rubric. It added six attributable observations (OBS-000038 through OBS-000043), including counterevidence. It created no opportunity, experiment, offer, or external action. A published price or subscription proves category pricing, not sales volume or demand for the proposed offer.

## OPP-0001 - Post-Discharge Family Care Arrangement Navigator

### Buyer

**PARTIAL.** Aging Life Care Association describes families hiring care managers privately by the hour (OBS-000038). That makes an adult child/family payer plausible, but no source shows them buying this narrower workflow.

### Willingness to pay

**WEAK.** The association's US$125-200/hour regional example demonstrates spend on professional navigation; it is not purchase evidence for OPP-0001.

### Current alternatives

**FACT:** Hospital discharge planning, post-acute referrals, care managers, agencies, and Wellthy are existing routes. CMS requires specified discharge-planning actions (OBS-000039).

### Switching and dissatisfaction

No direct switching evidence was found. **Against:** mandated discharge planning and professional services could be sufficient for many transitions.

### Recurrence

**PARTIAL.** Capacity and care-transition work recur in the upstream problem record, but a discharge event is episodic and no source established repeat purchase of this workflow.

### Reachability

**PARTIAL.** A care transition is an identifiable moment, but this pass did not validate a compliant, direct route to a family buyer at that moment.

### Regulatory and trust

**FACT:** CMS transition requirements create a baseline that the offer must complement, not misrepresent or replace. Medical, benefits, and local-service boundaries remain material trust constraints.

### Evidence for

- **FACT:** Private, family-paid care management is an established category (OBS-000038).
- **FACT:** Upstream evidence documents care-capacity burden and existing paid alternatives.
- **INFERENCE:** A narrower workflow may have a lower price and simpler purchase than full care management.

### Evidence against

- **FACT:** CMS discharge planning includes referrals and caregiver/representative participation (OBS-000039).
- **FACT:** Established care managers, agencies, and concierge services are credible substitutes.
- **UNKNOWN:** Whether families see a residual job worth paying for after those alternatives.

### Unknowns

Exactly one remaining unknown: will a recently discharged patient's adult child pay for a bounded non-clinical arrangement workflow instead of using discharge planning or a human service?

### Scoring dimension changes

| Dimension | Old | New | Reason |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Poor existing solutions | 5 | 4 | CMS baseline and paid human substitutes sharpen substitute risk. |
| All other dimensions | unchanged | unchanged | Family-paid navigation validates category spend and buyer plausibility, not the narrower offer's WTP. |

Score: **60 -> 59**. Confidence: **56 -> 64**. Readiness: **RESEARCH MORE**.

## OPP-0004 - Care Follow-Up Ownership Tracker

### Buyer

**PARTIAL.** App Store listings show a care-circle administrator can be the paid subscriber (OBS-000042); the practical buyer is likely an adult child administering family care. No direct purchase interview confirms this.

### Willingness to pay

**MODERATE at category level; WEAK for this offer.** A dementia-focused coordination app lists US$9.99/month or US$99.99/year after trial (OBS-000042). It does not establish conversion, retention, or willingness to switch to a narrow tracker.

### Current alternatives

**FACT:** Existing apps, portals, spreadsheets, calendars, and human services cover parts of the job. One Brelti reviewer described consolidating five apps into one (OBS-000040).

### Switching and dissatisfaction

**FACT:** A different Brelti reviewer objected to a required post-trial subscription (OBS-000041). This is one small-sample complaint, not a prevalence estimate. It suggests price friction, not proof that task ownership is unsolved.

### Recurrence

**STRONG problem recurrence.** Upstream observations describe recurring appointments, medications, referrals, and multi-person coordination. No new source establishes continued payment over time.

### Reachability

**PARTIAL.** Caregiver-app listings and public caregiver communities are reachable contexts, but this run did not prove efficient acquisition or a direct buyer channel.

### Regulatory and trust

The narrow non-medical positioning reduces clinical-claim risk, but health information and multi-party access create privacy and trust constraints. The reviewed apps themselves disclaim replacement of professional advice.

### Evidence for

- **FACT:** A published consumer coordination subscription exists (OBS-000042).
- **FACT:** One reviewer described multi-tool coordination friction and sibling use (OBS-000040).
- **FACT:** The upstream problem has recurring care-task evidence.

### Evidence against

- **FACT:** A small-sample Brelti review criticized a paid gate (OBS-000041).
- **FACT:** A Brelti review also indicates an incumbent can consolidate the proposed job.
- **UNKNOWN:** Whether enough families will switch from free, familiar tools.

### Unknowns

Exactly one remaining unknown: will an adult child who already uses a spreadsheet, portal, or caregiver app switch and pay specifically for unresolved follow-up ownership?

### Scoring dimension changes

| Dimension | Old | New | Reason |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Existing customer spending | 5 | 6 | Published consumer subscription makes category spend clearer. |
| Willingness to pay | 3 | 4 | A paid consumer price validates a price point, but not demand for this offer. |
| All other dimensions | unchanged | unchanged | The small review sample and strong substitutes do not justify stronger gap or reachability claims. |

Score: **55 -> 58**. Confidence: **59 -> 65**. Readiness: **RESEARCH MORE**.

## OPP-0002 - Paid Family-Care Arrangement Workflow

### Buyer

**PARTIAL.** An adult child, care recipient, or family group may pay, while eligible Medicaid participants use program supports. The economic buyer varies by state, eligibility, and family arrangement.

### Willingness to pay

**WEAK.** Upstream records show payment questions and a paid family-care arrangement. No source found a family paying for a non-legal organizing workflow before a professional service.

### Current alternatives

**FACT:** Medicaid self-direction can provide person-centered planning, broker/consultant support, and financial-management services for eligible participants (OBS-000043). Families also use lawyers, agencies, and informal agreements.

### Switching and dissatisfaction

No direct switching evidence was found. **Against:** eligible families may prefer specialized program supports; ineligible families may need legal, tax, benefits, or mediation expertise rather than software.

### Recurrence

**PARTIAL.** Caregiving administration can recur, but arrangement and pay decisions may be intermittent and state-program dependent.

### Reachability

**PARTIAL.** Public payment questions indicate a searchable moment, but the buyer, state program, and regulatory context fragment the channel.

### Regulatory and trust

**HIGH CONSTRAINT. FACT:** CMS describes state-plan/waiver pathways, service and budget plans, documentation, and financial-management services (OBS-000043). The offer must not provide legal, tax, benefits, or program eligibility advice.

### Evidence for

- **FACT:** Upstream observations contain several payment-related help requests and one paid family-care arrangement.
- **FACT:** Self-direction involves real administrative work, planning, and documentation (OBS-000043).
- **HYPOTHESIS:** A non-legal preparation workflow could reduce confusion before specialist contact.

### Evidence against

- **FACT:** Existing specialized supports include brokers/consultants and financial-management services for eligible participants (OBS-000043).
- **FACT:** State and program variation makes a generic workflow harder to productize safely.
- **UNKNOWN:** Whether a general workflow has enough value before professional advice.

### Unknowns

Exactly one remaining unknown: will a family facing paid-care questions pay for a non-legal organizing workflow before using an attorney, agency, or eligible program support?

### Scoring dimension changes

| Dimension | Old | New | Reason |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| Poor existing solutions | 4 | 3 | Existing specialized supports and state variation make a generic gap less clear. |
| All other dimensions | unchanged | unchanged | Complexity increases confidence in the caution, not evidence of standalone demand. |

Score: **54 -> 53**. Confidence: **53 -> 63**. Readiness: **RESEARCH MORE**.
