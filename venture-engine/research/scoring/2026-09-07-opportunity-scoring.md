## Evidence, uncertainty, and checks

- **OPP-0001:** Evidence: paid home care/concierge categories and recurring capacity burden. Against: agencies and Wellthy may be sufficient. Missing: buyer and transition-workflow willingness to pay.
- **OPP-0002:** Evidence: repeated caregiver-payment questions and one paid family arrangement. Against: legal, tax, benefits, and mediation needs. Missing: standalone workflow demand.
- **OPP-0003:** Evidence: work disruption and an established employer-benefit category. Against: no employer evidence and a strong incumbent. Missing: buyer demand and procurement viability.
- **OPP-0004:** Evidence: recurring multi-provider tasks and information-sharing request. Against: useful spreadsheets, portals, and low-cost alternatives. Missing: switching and paid-adoption evidence.
- **OPP-0005:** Evidence: bill, account, and coverage consequences. Against: automation/advisors may be safer and sufficient. Missing: direct demand, buyer clarity, and willingness to pay.

## Scorer critique

The fixed weights prevented existing spend from being mistaken for willingness to pay. The hardest dimensions were market size, poor existing solutions, and founder fit: all lack narrow, attributable evidence. Employer reachability and speed-to-validate made OPP-0003 materially weaker despite category spending. Before automation, require a source-backed buyer statement for willingness-to-pay and reachability scores, plus a defined rubric policy for `NOT READY` versus `RESEARCH MORE` below 60.
